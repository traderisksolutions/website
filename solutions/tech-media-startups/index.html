The sector

How tech, media and start-ups businesses buy insurance

Technology, media and start-up companies in Singapore buy insurance when an investor, an enterprise customer or a regulator asks. A term sheet asks for D&O before the round closes. An enterprise contract asks for technology errors and omissions and cyber at a stated limit before go-live. A media client asks for defamation and IP infringement cover. The PDPA requires a notifiable breach to be reported to the PDPC within 3 calendar days once assessed, with a penalty cap of 10 per cent of Singapore turnover.

The programme is not a single package. Technology E&O answers a customer claim that the software or service failed; cyber answers breach response, extortion and regulatory costs; D&O answers claims against founders and directors from investors, regulators or employees; group medical, keyman and business travel cover the team. E&O, cyber and D&O are claims-made and carry a retroactive date before which no work is covered. The gaps are usually a customer contract with limits above the policy, open-source and IP exclusions, and a D&O that stops at the Singapore entity while the parent is uninsured.

Named risks

The exposure, what it costs, the cover that answers it

Software or service failure
An outage or a defect costs an enterprise customer revenue and the customer claims under the contract. The limitation of liability clause is tested in court.
Technology errors and omissions at the limit the largest customer contract requires, with contractual liability not excluded.
Data breach or ransomware
User data is exfiltrated or systems are encrypted. The PDPC must be notified within 3 calendar days once the breach is assessed as notifiable; a breach affecting 500 or more individuals is notifiable on scale alone.
Cyber liability with breach response, extortion, business interruption and regulatory defence, at a limit sized to the user records held.
Investor or shareholder claim
A down round, a failed exit or a misstatement in the data room produces a claim against the founders and the board. The company cannot indemnify them until the claim is resolved.
D&O with Side A cover for individuals and entity cover for securities claims, scheduled to include the holding company and every subsidiary.
IP infringement or defamation
A media piece, an advertisement or a feature is alleged to infringe a copyright or trademark, or to defame a person. Injunction and defence costs come first.
Media liability or an intellectual property extension on the E&O policy, covering defence costs and damages for unintentional infringement.
Founder death or disability
A founder with the product knowledge or the investor relationships dies or is incapacitated. Investors trigger the key person clause.
Keyman insurance on the named founders at a sum that funds the replacement period the shareholders' agreement allows.
Employee claim
A dismissed employee alleges discrimination or wrongful termination. The claim names the founder personally.
Employment practices liability as a section of the D&O or management liability policy, with defence costs for the company and the individual.

Questions

Questions tech, media and start-ups buyers ask

When should a start-up buy D&O?

When the first outside investor joins the board, which is usually the seed or Series A term sheet. The investor's nominee director wants cover before taking the seat. The policy should name the holding company and every subsidiary, so a Singapore operating company with an overseas parent needs both scheduled. Limits rise with each round as the shareholder base and the claim exposure grow.

What is the difference between technology E&O and cyber insurance?

Technology E&O covers claims by customers that the product or service failed to perform: a bug, an outage, a missed specification. Cyber covers the costs of a security or privacy event: breach response, extortion, business interruption and regulatory action, plus claims by the people whose data was exposed. Many insurers combine both in one policy with a shared or separate limit.

Does cyber insurance pay PDPA penalties?

Most wordings cover regulatory defence costs and pay penalties where the law allows them to be insured. Whether a PDPC financial penalty is insurable in Singapore is not settled, so treat the penalty as uninsured and the defence and notification costs as insured. The penalty cap for contraventions from 1 October 2022 is 10 per cent of annual Singapore turnover where turnover exceeds S$10 million, or S$1 million otherwise.

Can one policy cover our Singapore and overseas entities?

Yes, if each entity is named and the territory and jurisdiction clauses cover where the claim can be brought. A policy limited to Singapore jurisdiction does not respond to a claim filed in a US court by a US customer. Enterprise contracts with overseas customers should be read against the policy's jurisdiction clause before signing.

What must we do after a data breach?

Assess whether the breach is notifiable within 30 calendar days of discovering it. If it causes or is likely to cause significant harm, or affects 500 or more individuals, notify the PDPC within 3 calendar days of that assessment and notify affected individuals as soon as practicable. Cyber policies require the insurer to be told at the same time and provide the forensic and legal panel that handles the response.

Sources: Personal Data Protection Act 2012, PDPC, Guide on Managing and Notifying Data Breaches under the PDPA, PDPC, required to notify the PDPC, MOM, work injury compensation insurance
