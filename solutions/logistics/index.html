The sector

How logistics and freight businesses buy insurance

Logistics companies in Singapore buy insurance because a customer, a port operator or a regulator asks for it. Shipper contracts demand proof of cargo liability cover. Prime movers and vans cannot be used on the road without third-party motor insurance under the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act. Drivers, packers and warehouse crew do manual work, so MOM requires work injury compensation insurance for each of them regardless of pay. Each non-Malaysian Work Permit holder also needs a S$60,000 medical policy and a S$5,000 security bond.

The programme is not a single policy. Cargo insurance pays the owner of the goods; forwarder liability pays claims against the operator; motor fleet covers the vehicles; work injury compensation covers the crew. Each is bought on a different basis: cargo on an open cover declared by shipment or turnover, liability on an annual limit with a deductible (the amount the insured pays first). The gap is usually goods in the warehouse between legs, or a sub-contracted haulier whose cover was never checked. The broker maps every handover and places each piece.

Named risks

The exposure, what it costs, the cover that answers it

Cargo loss in transit
A container is dropped, flooded or stolen between pick-up and delivery. The owner claims the invoice value plus freight, while the forwarder's trading conditions cap its own liability by weight.
Marine cargo on an annual open cover, Institute Cargo Clauses (A), with a per-conveyance limit above the largest consignment carried.
Forwarder liability
A mis-declared shipment, a wrong release or a customs delay produces a claim from the shipper or a fine from the authority. Standard trading conditions limit liability only if they were incorporated into the contract.
Freight forwarders liability with errors and omissions, fines and penalties, and a limit that matches the trading conditions in use.
Warehouse fire or theft
Customer stock held in a leased warehouse burns or is stolen. The customer claims under the storage contract, and the operator's own racking and handling equipment is a separate loss.
Warehouse legal liability for customer goods plus property cover on the operator's own fittings and equipment, with stock values declared monthly.
Fleet accidents
A prime mover injures a third party or damages a vehicle. Third-party death and bodily injury cover is compulsory under the Motor Vehicles Act; third-party property damage and own damage are not.
Comprehensive commercial fleet cover with third-party property damage and a fleet-wide deductible, not a per-vehicle third-party-only policy.
Worker injury
A packer or driver is injured at work. For accidents from 1 November 2025, WICA compensation reaches S$346,000 for permanent incapacity and S$53,000 for medical expenses, and an uninsured employer pays it.
Work injury compensation from an MOM-designated insurer for every manual worker, plus the S$60,000 medical policy for each Work Permit holder.
Customer insolvency
A large shipper stops paying freight invoices and enters liquidation. Thirty to ninety days of billed work becomes a bad debt.
Trade credit insurance on the receivables ledger with a credit limit set per buyer.

Questions

Questions logistics and freight buyers ask

Does my forwarder liability policy cover the cargo itself?

No. Forwarder liability pays claims made against the operator for its own errors, up to the limit in its trading conditions. The cargo owner recovers the full value only from a cargo policy. If the customer has none, the forwarder can arrange cargo cover as agent under its open cover and charge for it.

Is cargo insurance compulsory in Singapore?

No. The only compulsory covers for a logistics company are third-party motor insurance for each vehicle and work injury compensation for employees. Cargo cover is a contractual matter between the shipper, the forwarder and the carrier. Most shipper contracts and bank letters of credit require it.

Do I need WICA cover for drivers earning more than S$2,600 a month?

Yes. MOM requires work injury compensation insurance for all employees doing manual work regardless of salary, and for non-manual employees earning S$2,600 or less a month. Drivers, packers and forklift operators are manual workers. From 1 January 2021 the policy must come from an MOM-designated insurer. Failure to insure carries a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both.

What must the Work Permit medical policy cover?

At least S$60,000 a year per worker for inpatient care and day surgery. For policies from 1 July 2023 the insurer pays 75 per cent and the employer 25 per cent of claims above S$15,000. From 1 July 2025 exclusion clauses are standardised, premiums are set in two age bands, and insurers pay hospitals directly. The cost cannot be passed to the worker.

How is an open cargo cover declared?

Either by shipment, with a declaration per consignment, or by annual turnover, with a deposit premium adjusted at year end. The insurer sets a limit per conveyance and per location. Turnover declarations suit high-volume forwarders; per-shipment declarations suit project cargo and high-value consignments.
