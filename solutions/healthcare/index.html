The sector

How healthcare and life sciences businesses buy insurance

Clinics, hospitals, laboratories and biomedical companies in Singapore buy insurance because a licence, a hospital credentialing committee or a trial sponsor asks for it. The Healthcare Services Act replaced the Private Hospitals and Medical Clinics Act in three phases from 3 January 2022, and every licensee must meet its licensing conditions. Doctors need Singapore Medical Council registration and a practising certificate, and private hospitals require indemnity cover before granting admitting rights. HSA can make sponsor insurance a condition of a clinical trial authorisation.

The programme is not a single malpractice policy. Medical malpractice covers the practitioner and the entity for clinical negligence; public liability covers a patient who slips in the waiting room; cyber covers patient records and PDPA notification; electronic equipment covers imaging and laboratory instruments; products liability covers devices and consumables sold. Malpractice is claims-made: the policy in force when the claim is made responds, for treatment after the retroactive date. The gaps are usually locum doctors assumed to be covered, run-off when a practitioner retires, and research work outside the clinical policy.

Named risks

The exposure, what it costs, the cover that answers it

Clinical negligence claim
A patient alleges a missed diagnosis, a surgical error or a wrong prescription. Defence costs run from the first letter of demand through SMC complaint and civil claim.
Medical malpractice on a claims-made basis covering the entity and each named practitioner, with SMC disciplinary defence costs included.
Patient data breach
Clinical records are exposed. The PDPC must be notified within 3 calendar days once the breach is assessed as notifiable, and a breach affecting 500 or more individuals is notifiable on scale alone.
Cyber liability with breach response, notification, forensics and regulatory defence, at a limit sized to the number of patient records held.
Equipment failure
An MRI, CT scanner or analyser fails from an electrical fault or is damaged by water. Replacement is months away and bookings move to a competitor.
Electronic equipment insurance on an all-risks basis at reinstatement value, with a business interruption extension for lost fees.
Clinical trial injury
A trial participant suffers an adverse event. The sponsor and the site face compensation claims, and HSA approval of the trial can carry an insurance condition.
Clinical trials liability per protocol, naming sponsor, site and investigators, for the trial duration and a tail period for late-emerging injury.
Product defect
A device, implant or consumable fails in use. The manufacturer, the importer and the distributor are each drawn into the claim.
Products liability with a limit the hospital purchasing contract accepts and a recall extension for the cost of withdrawing the batch.
Practitioner retirement or departure
A doctor leaves the practice and a claim arrives two years later for treatment given while employed. The current policy covers only current staff.
Run-off cover for departing practitioners, or an entity policy that covers former practitioners for work done while employed.

Questions

Questions healthcare and life sciences buyers ask

Is medical indemnity compulsory for doctors in Singapore?

No statute sets a minimum indemnity limit for doctors. Every doctor must be registered with the Singapore Medical Council and hold a practising certificate. In practice the employer, the private hospital granting admitting rights and the HCSA licensee set the requirement, and most require cover before a doctor sees patients. Indemnity comes either from a medical defence organisation or from an insurer's malpractice policy.

What is run-off cover?

A claims-made malpractice policy covers claims made while it is in force. When a practitioner retires or a clinic closes, claims can still arrive for earlier treatment. Run-off cover keeps the policy responding to those claims for a set number of years after the practice stops. Without it, the practitioner is uninsured for the tail.

Are locum doctors covered under the clinic's policy?

Only if the policy says so. Many entity policies cover employed practitioners and exclude locums and visiting consultants, who are expected to hold their own cover. A clinic that uses locums should either have them named or have the policy extended to cover any practitioner working under its licence, and should collect proof of each locum's own indemnity.

What must a clinic do after a patient data breach?

Assess whether the breach is notifiable within 30 calendar days. Health records are likely to cause significant harm if exposed, and a breach affecting 500 or more individuals is notifiable on scale. Notify the PDPC within 3 calendar days of that assessment and the affected patients as soon as practicable. The penalty cap is 10 per cent of annual Singapore turnover where turnover exceeds S$10 million, or S$1 million otherwise.

Does a clinic need public liability as well as malpractice?

Yes. Malpractice covers injury arising from clinical treatment. Public liability covers injury and property damage that has nothing to do with treatment: a fall in the waiting room, a water leak into the unit below, a visitor hit by a trolley. The two policies have different triggers, and a claim that falls between them is paid by the clinic.
