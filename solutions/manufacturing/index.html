The sector

How manufacturing businesses buy insurance

Manufacturers in Singapore buy insurance because a bank, a landlord, a customer or MOM requires it. A factory lease requires fire cover; an equipment loan requires the lender named on the machinery policy; a customer's supply agreement requires product liability and recall at a set limit. Every production worker does manual work under WICA, so work injury compensation is compulsory regardless of pay. Manufacturing was 17.3 per cent of Singapore's GDP in 2024, and most of that value sits in plant that cannot be replaced quickly.

The programme is not a shop package. Property covers buildings, plant and stock at reinstatement value; machinery breakdown covers the internal electrical and mechanical failure that fire policies exclude; business interruption pays gross profit for an indemnity period (the months the insurer keeps paying); products liability covers injury and damage from goods sold; stock throughput follows raw material and finished goods in transit and storage. The gaps are usually plant valued at book rather than replacement cost, an indemnity period shorter than the lead time for a replacement line, and a recall that nobody insured.

Named risks

The exposure, what it costs, the cover that answers it

Factory fire
A fire in a dust-laden or solvent-using process destroys the line and the building. The sum insured was the depreciated book value, and the average clause cuts the claim in proportion.
Property on an all-risks basis at full reinstatement value, with values reviewed after every capital purchase.
Machinery breakdown
A CNC centre, press or injection moulding machine fails from an internal electrical or mechanical fault. The fire policy excludes it and the manufacturer's warranty has expired.
Machinery breakdown scheduled by machine at replacement value, with business interruption following breakdown as an extension.
Lost production
The line is down for six months while a replacement machine is built overseas. Rent, salaries and the loan continue, and the customer moves the order.
Business interruption on gross profit with an indemnity period at least equal to the longest lead time, plus increased cost of working for outsourced production.
Product liability and recall
A component fails in a customer's product and the customer recalls the whole batch. The supply agreement passes the recall cost back to the manufacturer.
Products liability at the limit the supply agreement requires, with a recall expenses extension and the territory set to match where the goods are sold.
Worker injury
An operator is injured at a press or during maintenance. For accidents from 1 November 2025, WICA compensation reaches S$346,000 for permanent incapacity and S$269,000 for death.
Work injury compensation from an MOM-designated insurer for every manual worker, with the Work Permit medical policy and security bond per foreign worker.
Stock in transit and storage
Imported raw material is damaged at sea, or finished goods are lost between the factory and the customer. Each leg sits with a different carrier and a different limit.
Stock throughput covering raw material, work in progress and finished goods from supplier to customer, on one valuation basis.

Questions

Questions manufacturing buyers ask

Does fire insurance cover machinery breakdown?

No. Fire and property all-risks policies exclude loss caused by mechanical or electrical breakdown, even when the breakdown then causes a fire that is covered. Breakdown needs its own machinery breakdown policy, scheduled by machine, and a business interruption extension if lost output matters.

How is business interruption calculated?

On gross profit: turnover less the purchases and variable costs that stop when production stops. The insurer pays the reduction in gross profit during the indemnity period, plus increased costs of working such as outsourcing or overtime. The sum insured is the annual gross profit multiplied by the indemnity period in years, so a 24-month period needs twice the annual figure.

Do we need product recall cover separately?

Yes. Products liability pays third-party injury and damage after a defective product causes harm. It does not pay the cost of withdrawing the product from the market, which a recall expenses extension or a standalone recall policy covers. Supply agreements with larger customers often pass recall costs to the supplier, so the limit should match the contract.

Which employees must have WICA cover?

All employees doing manual work regardless of salary, and all non-manual employees earning S$2,600 or less a month, local or foreign. From 1 January 2021 the policy must be from an MOM-designated insurer. Independent contractors are not covered. Failure to insure carries a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both.

What cover do Work Permit holders on the line need?

A medical policy of at least S$60,000 a year per worker, submitted before the permit is issued or renewed, and a S$5,000 security bond for each non-Malaysian worker. For policies from 1 July 2023 the insurer pays 75 per cent and the employer 25 per cent of claims above S$15,000. The cost cannot be passed to the worker. WICA cover applies on top.
