The sector

How professional services businesses buy insurance

Professional firms in Singapore buy insurance because a regulator or a client engagement letter requires it. Law practices must hold professional indemnity of S$1 million for sole proprietors and partnerships and S$2 million for law corporations and LLPs under the Law Society scheme. Licensed financial advisers must hold S$1 million. Consultants, architects, engineers and accountants face the same demand from clients, whose procurement terms ask for a professional indemnity certificate before a contract is signed.

The programme is not only professional indemnity. PI answers client claims for negligent advice, errors or missed deadlines. D&O answers claims against partners and directors as individuals. Cyber answers a breach of client files and the PDPA duties that follow. Group medical and benefits meet the employment contracts. PI and D&O are claims-made: the policy in force when the claim is made responds, for work after the retroactive date. The gaps are usually a retroactive date set at inception rather than founding, a per-claim limit that is also the annual aggregate, and no run-off when a partner leaves.

Named risks

The exposure, what it costs, the cover that answers it

Negligent advice
A client acts on a report, a valuation or a tax position that proves wrong and claims its loss. Defence costs start before liability is decided.
Professional indemnity with defence costs in addition to the limit and a retroactive date at the firm's founding.
Missed deadline or lost document
A filing is missed, a title deed or a client's original records are lost. The client claims the cost of reconstruction and the loss that followed.
Professional indemnity with a loss of documents extension and a limit per claim that is not also the annual aggregate.
Client data breach
Client files are exfiltrated through a compromised mailbox. The PDPC must be notified within 3 calendar days once the breach is assessed as notifiable, and the penalty cap is 10 per cent of Singapore turnover.
Cyber liability with breach response, notification and regulatory defence, at a limit sized to the number of client records held.
Claim against a partner or director
A regulator, a former partner or an employee brings a claim against an individual for a management decision rather than a professional act.
D&O or management liability covering the partners and directors, with employment practices liability added for staff claims.
Loss of a key partner
The rainmaker or the sole signatory dies or is incapacitated. Fee income falls and the bank calls the overdraft.
Keyman insurance on the named partners at a sum that funds the transition and the bank's requirement.
Staff attrition
A competitor offers a richer benefits package and the associates move. Recruitment and ramp-up costs follow.
Group medical, dental and term life on a schedule that matches the market the firm hires from.

Questions

Questions professional services buyers ask

What professional indemnity limit does a Singapore law practice need?

Under section 75A of the Legal Profession Act and the Law Society scheme, S$1 million for each and every claim for sole proprietorships and partnerships, and S$2 million for each and every claim for law corporations and limited liability law partnerships, inclusive of costs. The Law Society must be satisfied the cover is in place before a practising certificate is issued. Many firms buy top-up cover above the minimum.

What does claims-made mean for my professional indemnity policy?

The policy responds to claims first made against the firm during the policy period, regardless of when the work was done, as long as the work was after the retroactive date. A gap in cover resets the retroactive date and leaves earlier work uninsured. Renewing without a break keeps the original date, which should be the firm's founding date.

Do I need D&O if I am a small partnership?

Professional indemnity covers professional acts. It does not cover claims against the partners for management decisions: a dispute with a departing partner, an employee claim for wrongful dismissal, a regulator's inquiry into the firm's accounts. D&O or management liability covers those. The need depends on headcount, the number of partners and the regulator, not on revenue.

Does professional indemnity cover a data breach?

Usually not. Professional indemnity covers claims by clients for negligent professional services. The costs of a breach are first-party: forensics, notification, legal advice on the PDPA, credit monitoring and regulatory defence. Cyber insurance covers those and the third-party claims that follow. Some PI wordings add a small cyber extension, which is rarely enough.

What happens to cover when a partner retires?

The firm's policy continues to cover work the partner did while at the firm, as long as the firm keeps renewing. If the firm closes or merges, run-off cover keeps the policy responding to claims for a set number of years. The Law Society scheme covers former practitioners of a law practice for work done there; other professions need run-off written into the policy.

Sources: Law Society of Singapore, compulsory professional indemnity insurance, MAS, financial adviser's licence requirements, Personal Data Protection Act 2012, PDPC, Guide on Managing and Notifying Data Breaches under the PDPA
