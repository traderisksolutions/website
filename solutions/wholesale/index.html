The sector

How wholesale and distribution businesses buy insurance

Wholesalers and distributors in Singapore buy insurance when a landlord, a bank or a principal asks. A warehouse lease requires fire cover on the premises. An inventory financing line requires the bank's interest noted on the stock policy. A foreign principal's distribution agreement requires product liability at a stated limit. Stock is the largest asset on the balance sheet and it moves daily between the port, the warehouse and the customer. Credit terms of 30 to 90 days put a second large asset, the receivables ledger, at risk.

A distributor's programme is not a shop package. Stock is insured either on separate cargo and warehouse policies or on one stock throughput policy that follows goods from the supplier's door to the customer's, valued at cost plus a stated uplift. Products liability sits on the public liability policy and must name the goods sold. Trade credit insures receivables against a buyer's insolvency or protracted default. The gaps are usually stock in a third-party warehouse, goods delivered but unpaid, and peak-season stock above the declared sum insured.

Named risks

The exposure, what it costs, the cover that answers it

Warehouse fire
A fire in a shared industrial building destroys the stock and the racking. The declared sum insured was set in January and the December stock was double.
Property or stock throughput on a declaration basis with a peak-season limit, so the sum insured follows the stock rather than the calendar.
Stock in transit
An import container is lost overboard, or a local delivery van is hijacked. The supplier was paid on shipment and the customer has not taken delivery.
Stock throughput or marine cargo on an open cover, Institute Cargo Clauses (A), covering both the inbound ocean leg and local distribution.
Product liability claim
A distributed product injures a consumer or damages property. The customer who bought from the distributor claims against the distributor under the sale contract, whoever made the goods.
Products liability on an occurrence basis at the limit the principal's agreement requires, with the product range named in the schedule.
Buyer default
A major retailer or reseller enters judicial management owing 60 days of invoices. The distributor has already paid the principal for the goods.
Trade credit insurance with a credit limit per buyer and cover for both insolvency and protracted default, usually at 85 to 90 per cent of the invoice.
Business interruption after a loss
The warehouse is unusable for four months after a fire. Rent, salaries and the principal's minimum purchase commitment continue without sales.
Business interruption on gross profit with an indemnity period long enough to find, fit out and restock a replacement warehouse.
Worker injury
A picker or forklift driver is injured. Warehouse staff do manual work, so WICA cover is compulsory regardless of salary, with medical expenses up to S$53,000 per accident.
Work injury compensation from an MOM-designated insurer for every manual worker and every non-manual employee earning S$2,600 or less a month.

Questions

Questions wholesale and distribution buyers ask

Should I buy stock throughput or separate cargo and warehouse policies?

Stock throughput suits a distributor whose stock is in motion most of the time and who holds it in more than one location. One policy, one insurer and one valuation basis remove the argument over which policy pays when goods are damaged on the loading bay. Separate policies suit a business with a single warehouse and few inbound shipments.

What does trade credit insurance pay?

A percentage of the unpaid invoice, usually 85 to 90 per cent, when a covered buyer becomes insolvent or fails to pay within the protracted default period set in the policy. Each buyer has a credit limit set by the insurer. Sales above the limit are uninsured, so the limit must be reviewed as a customer grows.

Am I liable for a product I only distribute?

Yes. The customer's contract is with the distributor, so the first claim for a defective product lands on the distributor under the sale contract. The distributor may recover from the manufacturer, but a foreign maker can be slow or beyond reach. Products liability cover responds to the claim; a recall extension pays the cost of withdrawing the batch.

How do I insure stock that peaks before festive seasons?

Insure on a declaration basis. The policy carries a maximum limit set at the peak and the premium is adjusted on monthly or quarterly stock declarations. A fixed sum insured set at the average leaves the peak under-insured, and the average clause then reduces every claim in proportion.

Does my fire policy cover stock in a third-party warehouse?

Only if that location is named in the schedule. A fire policy covers the premises listed. Stock at a 3PL, a bonded warehouse or a consignment customer needs the location added or a stock throughput policy that covers goods anywhere in Singapore within a per-location limit.
