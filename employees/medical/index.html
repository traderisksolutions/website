The cover

Group medical insurance in Singapore

Group medical insurance is a hospital and surgical policy an employer buys for its staff, usually with outpatient GP, specialist and dental riders. In Singapore it is voluntary for local employees and Employment Pass holders, and compulsory in a different form for Work Permit and S Pass holders. The trigger is hiring: an offer letter that promises medical benefits, a parent company group standard, a tender that asks for it, or an investor checklist. Companies from two employees upwards buy it.

It is not a substitute for MediShield Life, which stays in force, and it is not work injury cover. The policy is annual, priced per head by plan tier and age, with room and board set by ward class and an annual limit per employee. Most plans are issued on a group basis without individual medical questions above a minimum headcount. The broker benchmarks the plan against the market, tests the insurer's wording on pre-existing conditions and portability, and runs the renewal on claims data.

Who buys it

  • Start-ups and SMEs from two employees writing a first benefits package
  • Regional headquarters matching a parent company plan for Singapore staff
  • Employers of Work Permit and S Pass holders who want one plan above the MOM minimum
  • Firms with ageing or long-serving staff where individual cover would be declined or loaded
  • Employers using the Portable Medical Benefits Scheme or TMIS to lift the tax deduction to 2%
  • Companies adding dependants, dental or outpatient riders at renewal

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Employee census with date of birth, gender, pass type and plan tier
  • Current benefit schedule and policy wording
  • Two to three years of claims experience by benefit
  • Dependants to be included and who pays for them
  • Employee handbook wording on medical benefits
  • Whether a portable scheme (PMBS, TMIS or Shield plan) is in place

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Ward class and annual limit
A private hospital single-bed plan with a S$200,000 limit costs several times a B1 ward plan.
Age profile
Insurers rate by age band, so a workforce over 50 pays more per head than a young one.
Headcount
Larger groups get experience rating and fewer medical questions; very small groups follow book rates.
Claims ratio
A loss ratio above the insurer's target leads to a loading or a plan redesign at renewal.
Riders
Outpatient GP, specialist, dental and maternity riders each add a per-head premium and drive claims frequency.
Co-payment and deductible
A 10% co-payment or a per-visit deductible cuts premium and claim volume.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Promising benefits the policy does not deliver
A handbook that says "full medical cover" and a policy with a S$20,000 limit leave the employer paying the gap. Align the handbook to the schedule and state the ward class, limit and co-payment in writing.
Ignoring the IRAS cap
Medical expenses, including group medical premiums, are deductible only up to 1% of total employee remuneration, or 2% with a qualifying portable scheme; the excess is disallowed. Model the cap before adding riders, and consider PMBS or TMIS if you are close to it.
Assuming pre-existing conditions are covered
Many plans exclude or impose waiting periods on pre-existing conditions for small groups and late joiners. Ask for the exact wording and the free cover limit before enrolling staff, and declare known conditions where the insurer asks.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Is group medical insurance compulsory in Singapore?

Not for local employees or Employment Pass holders. It is compulsory for Work Permit and S Pass holders, who must have inpatient and day surgery cover of at least S$60,000 a year under MOM rules. Separately, the Employment Act gives eligible employees up to 14 days of paid outpatient sick leave and 60 days of paid hospitalisation leave after three months of service.

How much of the premium can the company deduct?

Medical expenses, which IRAS defines to include premiums on medical and dental insurance, are deductible up to 1% of total employee remuneration for the year. The cap rises to 2% if the employer runs the Portable Medical Benefits Scheme, the Transferable Medical Insurance Scheme or provides portable Shield plans. Spend above the cap is not deductible.

What makes a plan portable?

Under PMBS the employer pays an extra MediSave contribution of at least 1% of gross monthly salary, minimum S$16 a month, for at least 20% of local employees and every new local hire. Under TMIS the group hospital plan keeps inpatient cover for up to 12 months after an employee leaves, for at least 50% of local employees. A Shield plan option covers at least 20% of local staff.

Are pre-existing conditions covered?

It depends on the wording and the group size. Larger groups are usually accepted without medical questions and pre-existing conditions are covered from day one; smaller groups and late entrants may face exclusions or a waiting period. Insurers also set a free cover limit, the benefit issued without underwriting. Get the clause in writing before enrolment.

Is the premium taxable in my employees' hands?

IRAS treats employer-paid insurance as a benefit-in-kind, but applies a concession for group medical insurance provided in place of medical costs the employer would otherwise reimburse, where the benefit is available to all staff. Under the concession the premium is not taxed on the employee. The concession does not apply to investment holding companies or cost-plus service companies.

How small can the group be?

Insurer packaged schemes in Singapore start at two full-time employees. Below the insurer's threshold, usually around 10 to 20 lives, staff are enrolled on a packaged plan at book rates with limited choice of ward class and riders. Above it the plan is customised and rated on the group's own claims experience.
