The cover

Executive D&O insurance in Singapore

Directors and officers (D&O) insurance pays the defence costs, settlements and awards a director or officer faces personally for a wrongful act in managing the company: a breach of duty, a misstatement, an employment claim, a regulatory investigation. Private companies in Singapore buy it when an investor joins the board, when a lender or customer asks, when a subsidiary director is appointed overseas, or after a dispute between shareholders. The Companies Act voids most indemnities the company could give instead, so insurance is the usual route.

It is not professional indemnity for the firm's services and it does not pay fines where the law prohibits it. The policy is claims-made: it responds to claims first made during the policy year, for acts after the retroactive date. It is bought with an aggregate limit shared by all insureds, a retention that applies to company reimbursement but usually not to individuals, and a run-off option that extends reporting after a sale or liquidation. The broker sets the limit, negotiates the conduct and insured-versus-insured wording and manages notifications.

Who buys it

  • Venture-backed companies whose term sheet requires D&O cover for investor directors
  • Private companies with external, nominee or independent directors
  • Regional holding companies appointing directors to overseas subsidiaries
  • Firms in a shareholder dispute or a change of control
  • Companies employing more than 50 staff, where employment claims are the most frequent trigger
  • Not-for-profits, societies and charities with volunteer boards

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Latest audited accounts and current management accounts
  • Group structure with every entity and directorship to be insured
  • Shareholder register and any shareholders' agreement
  • Headcount and any employment disputes in the past five years
  • Any claims, investigations or circumstances known to the board
  • Planned transactions: fundraising, sale, listing, restructuring

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Relying on an indemnity in the constitution
Section 172 of the Companies Act makes void any provision that exempts or indemnifies an officer against liability for negligence, default, breach of duty or breach of trust to the company. Section 172A lets the company buy and maintain insurance for that liability instead, so place the policy rather than rely on the clause.
Letting the retroactive date move
Switching insurer with a new retroactive date drops cover for every act before it. Keep continuity of the retroactive date in writing at each renewal and notify known circumstances before the old policy expires.
No run-off after a sale
When the company is sold or wound up the policy ends and the former directors have no cover for claims made later. Buy run-off at completion; the cost is a one-off premium and the reporting period should outlast the limitation period for claims.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Is D&O insurance compulsory for a Singapore private company?

No. The Companies Act does not require it, but section 172A expressly permits a company to purchase and maintain insurance for an officer against liability that section 172 forbids the company to indemnify. Investor term sheets, lender covenants and customer contracts frequently require it, which is why most private companies with outside shareholders hold a policy.

What are Side A, B and C?

Side A pays the individual director directly where the company cannot or will not indemnify, for example in insolvency; it usually carries no retention. Side B reimburses the company for the indemnity it is permitted to pay. Side C covers the company itself for securities claims and matters mainly for listed companies. Private companies buy A and B; C is added before a listing.

What does a private company D&O policy exclude?

Dishonest or fraudulent conduct, usually only once established by final judgment or admission; claims pending or litigation known before the policy; claims by one insured against another, with carve-backs; bodily injury and property damage; and public offerings unless endorsed. Fines and penalties are paid only where the law allows. Read the conduct and insured-versus-insured clauses before binding.

Does it cover regulatory investigations?

Yes, where the wording defines investigation to include an officer named in writing by a regulator, including as a witness. The policy pays legal representation costs for attendance before ACRA, MAS, MOM, PDPC or the police, and some wordings include pre-investigation costs for an internal inquiry that leads to a self-report. Fines imposed on the individual are covered only where insurable at law.

Are employment claims included?

Often, as an employment practices liability extension with its own sub-limit, S$1 million in one Singapore product. It covers wrongful dismissal, discrimination and harassment claims against the company and its officers. For a private company this is the most frequent claim type, so price the extension at every renewal.

What limit should a private company buy?

Singapore products for private companies quote from S$500,000 to S$2 million on simplified terms by revenue band, and up to S$5 million on full underwriting below set asset thresholds. The limit is shared by every insured person and by defence costs, so a company with several outside directors or an overseas subsidiary needs more than a founder-only board.
