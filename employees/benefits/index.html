The cover

Employee benefits insurance in Singapore

Employee benefits insurance is the package of group policies an employer funds for staff: term life, personal accident, hospital and surgical, outpatient, dental and critical illness. In Singapore it sits on top of the statutory layer of CPF, work injury compensation and Employment Act leave. The trigger is usually a hiring plan, a parent company standard, a collective agreement or an investor due diligence list. Companies from two employees buy packaged schemes; larger firms build a custom programme.

It is not a pension and it is not a replacement for the compulsory work injury or foreign worker medical policies. Each line is an annual group policy, priced per head, with sums assured set as a multiple of salary or a flat amount and a free cover limit below which no medical evidence is needed. The broker designs the tiers, places each line with the insurer best priced for it, aligns the policy wording with the staff handbook and manages joiners, leavers and claims through the year.

Who buys it

  • Start-ups and SMEs from two employees writing their first benefits package
  • Regional offices that must match a group benefits standard in Singapore
  • Firms under a collective agreement with insured death and disability benefits
  • Employers replacing ad hoc reimbursements with an insured, budgeted plan
  • Professional practices that need cover for partners and staff alike
  • Companies preparing for a funding round or acquisition due diligence

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Employee census with date of birth, gender, salary band and occupation
  • Benefit design by tier: sums assured, ward class, limits, riders
  • Existing policy schedules and renewal dates for each line
  • Claims experience for the past two to three years
  • Staff handbook or collective agreement wording on benefits
  • Headcount forecast for the policy year

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Lines included
Term life and personal accident are cheap per head; hospital and outpatient riders carry most of the cost.
Sum assured basis
A 36-month salary multiple costs more than a flat S$100,000, and sums above the free cover limit need medical evidence.
Age and occupation profile
Older groups and manual occupations are rated higher on both life and accident lines.
Headcount
Packaged schemes for under 50 lives use book rates; larger groups are rated on their own experience.
Claims history
Two to three years of medical claims data sets the renewal for the hospital and outpatient lines.
Dependant cover
Adding spouses and children raises the medical premium and the claim count.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Mismatch between handbook and policy
The handbook promises a benefit the policy limits or excludes, and the employer funds the difference. Write the handbook from the policy schedule, not the other way round, and update both at renewal.
Missing the free cover limit
A senior hire with a sum assured above the free cover limit is enrolled without medical evidence and the claim is declined. Check the limit at every joiner, and get the medical done before the cover is confirmed.
Claiming a deduction and taxing staff twice
For group policies other than medical, the premium is a taxable benefit to the employee unless the employer elects not to claim the deduction, and the election must cover every employee on the policy. Decide the treatment line by line with your tax adviser before the first payroll.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What does a typical Singapore programme contain?

Group term life for death and total permanent disability, group personal accident for accidental death and disablement, group hospital and surgical with a major medical rider, and outpatient GP and specialist riders. Dental, critical illness, long-term disability and dependant cover are added by tier. Work injury compensation and foreign worker medical insurance are bought separately because MOM sets their terms.

Can the company deduct the premiums?

Yes for group policies where employees or their nominees are the beneficiaries, and from year of assessment 2019 also where the employer is the named beneficiary with no obligation to pass on the payout; those payouts are then taxable to the employer. Premiums on policies with a cash surrender or investment value are capital and not deductible. Medical premiums fall under the 1% or 2% medical expenses cap.

Are the premiums taxed on employees?

IRAS treats employer-paid insurance as a benefit-in-kind. Two concessions apply: group medical provided in place of reimbursed medical costs and open to all staff, and other group insurance where the employer elects not to claim the premium as a deduction. The election must apply to every employee covered. Investment holding and cost-plus service companies are outside the concessions.

Is there a minimum number of employees?

Packaged schemes from Singapore insurers start at two full-time employees, with term life and hospital cover issued without medical examination up to a stated free cover limit. In the SME schemes we reviewed the limit is S$200,000 for term life. Above roughly 50 employees the insurer prices a customised plan on the group's own data.

What happens when an employee leaves?

Cover ends on the last day of service unless the policy includes a conversion option, which lets the employee buy an individual policy without medical evidence within a set period, or the employer runs TMIS, which keeps hospital cover for up to 12 months after departure. Ask for both clauses when the plan is placed.

How does this sit with CPF and statutory benefits?

CPF, work injury compensation and Employment Act leave are the statutory floor; the insured programme sits above them. Eligible employees already get 14 days of paid outpatient sick leave and 60 days of paid hospitalisation leave after three months of service. The insured plan pays the hospital bill and the lump sums that the statutory layer does not.
