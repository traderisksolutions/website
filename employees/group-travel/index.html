The cover

Group business travel insurance in Singapore

Group business travel insurance is an annual policy that covers every employee the company sends overseas, without naming them in advance. It pays overseas medical bills, emergency evacuation and repatriation, trip cancellation, delays, lost equipment and personal liability while the employee is travelling for work. Singapore companies buy it when staff start flying regionally, when a client or parent company asks for it, or after one uninsured hospital bill abroad. It is bought by firms from two employees upwards.

It is not work injury compensation: an accident on an overseas assignment is already a WICA claim, with medical expenses capped at S$53,000. Travel cover adds illness, evacuation and the non-medical losses. The policy is unnamed, priced per traveller or on declared trip days, with a maximum trip length, a regional or worldwide territory, and a personal deviation allowance for leisure days around a trip. The broker sets the territory, limits and leisure extension, and handles emergency assistance and claims.

Who buys it

  • Regional sales and service teams travelling to ASEAN, India and China every month
  • Project and engineering firms sending staff on site assignments of weeks at a time
  • Head offices whose directors and C-suite travel worldwide, including the USA and Canada
  • Firms whose client contracts require insured, evacuation-ready travellers
  • Companies with staff over 70, where individual travel policies decline or load
  • Employers sending staff to locations without hospitals able to treat serious injury

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Estimated number of travellers and trips per year by region
  • Longest expected trip and any assignments above 90 days
  • Occupations and activities undertaken on trips
  • Age profile of frequent travellers
  • Current travel policy and claims for the past three years
  • Whether leisure days or accompanying family should be included

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Territory
Worldwide including the USA and Canada is rated above a regional Asia-Pacific scope.
Medical and evacuation limits
Moving overseas medical from S$250,000 to S$600,000 adds premium; evacuation is usually unlimited.
Trip volume
Premium is per traveller for frequent flyers, or per trip-day for occasional travel declared at year end.
Pre-existing condition rider
Extending cover to known conditions adds a per-head loading but removes the most common declined claim.
Occupation
Office-based travellers are standard; site, offshore or security work moves the risk to a different class.
Leisure extension
Covering personal days around a trip, or pure leisure travel for executives, adds premium or is included for C-suite only.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Relying on personal cards or individual policies
Card cover often needs the fare paid on the card and stops at modest medical limits; individual policies are bought trip by trip and missed. An annual unnamed group policy covers every trip automatically and the finance team buys nothing per trip.
Exceeding the trip length
Policies cap each business trip, 183 days in the wording we reviewed, and cover stops on day 184. Flag any assignment above the cap before departure so the broker can arrange an extension or an expatriate medical plan.
Undeclared pre-existing conditions
Most wordings exclude pre-existing conditions unless a rider is bought, and a cardiac event abroad becomes an uninsured S$100,000 bill. Buy the rider for the whole group, or choose a wording that covers pre-existing conditions as standard.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Is business travel insurance compulsory in Singapore?

No law requires it. MOM requires work injury compensation insurance, which does respond to an accident on an overseas assignment, but only on the WICA scale and only for accidents, not illness. Travel cover is bought because a hospital stay or evacuation abroad can exceed those limits several times over, and because clients and parent companies ask for proof.

How long can a single trip last?

Wordings set a maximum per trip; the Singapore product we reviewed allows 183 days, inclusive of up to 31 days of personal deviation before, during or after the business trip. Trips of inter-city travel over 50 km count as business trips. Assignments longer than the cap need an extension or an expatriate plan.

Does it cover leisure days on a work trip?

Usually yes, up to a stated number of days, 31 in the wording reviewed, when they are attached to a business trip. Pure leisure travel for C-suite staff is included as an extension in some products at no extra premium; for other employees it is an optional add-on priced per head.

What if an employee falls ill abroad?

The policy pays overseas medical expenses up to the plan limit, commonly S$100,000 to S$600,000 per person, and emergency medical evacuation and repatriation, usually without limit, arranged through the insurer's 24-hour assistance line. Post-trip medical expenses in Singapore are covered up to a separate sub-limit. Call assistance first; direct admission without a guarantee of payment slows the claim.

Are pre-existing conditions covered?

Standard wordings exclude them. Insurers offer a pre-existing condition rider that extends overseas medical and evacuation to known conditions, and some corporate products include pre-existing conditions without a rider. For a group with older or frequent travellers the rider is the single most useful extension.

Who is eligible and how small can the group be?

Products in Singapore enrol from two employees on an unnamed basis, with insured persons aged 16 to 70 at entry and renewable to 75 in the wording we reviewed; some products cover beyond 80 at reduced sums. The insured must be resident in Singapore. The company declares headcount or trip days and the insurer adjusts at renewal.
