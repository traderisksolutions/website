The cover

Keyman insurance in Singapore

Keyman insurance is a life, total permanent disability or critical illness policy that a company buys on a founder, director or rainmaker, with the company as owner and beneficiary. The payout replaces lost profit, funds a replacement hire or settles debt while the business recovers. In Singapore the purchase is usually forced by a bank that wants the guarantor of a loan insured, by an investor term sheet, or by a shareholders' agreement that needs cash to buy out a deceased partner's shares.

It is not an employee benefit; the key person and the family receive nothing from the policy. The cover is a term policy with a fixed sum assured and term, renewable yearly or level for a fixed term, with no cash or investment value if the premium is to be tax-deductible. Sizing follows the profit attributable to the person, the loan balance, or the agreed share value. The broker obtains quotes across life insurers, manages the medical underwriting, and documents the basis for the sum assured for the tax computation.

Who buys it

  • Founder-led SMEs where one person brings in most of the revenue
  • Borrowers whose bank requires cover on the director who guarantees the facility
  • Partnerships and multi-shareholder companies funding a buy-sell agreement
  • Technology and professional firms built around one technical lead or licence holder
  • Companies with an investor term sheet that requires key person cover
  • Family businesses planning succession across a generation

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Name, date of birth and role of the key person, with a signed consent
  • Basis of the sum assured: attributable profit, loan agreement or share valuation
  • Last two years of audited accounts
  • Shareholding structure and any buy-sell or shareholders' agreement
  • Bank facility letter if the lender requires the cover
  • Health declaration and any existing life cover on the person

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Age and health of the key person
Premium rises with age and any loading from the medical; a non-smoker rate is markedly lower.
Sum assured
The sum is set from attributable profit, loan balance or share value; a higher sum raises premium in proportion.
Term and renewability
A level 10-year term costs more each year at the start than a yearly renewable policy but does not rise with age.
Benefits included
Adding total permanent disability or critical illness to the death benefit adds premium and underwriting.
Occupation and travel
Hazardous occupations or frequent travel to high-risk countries attract a loading or an exclusion.
Policy type
A policy with cash value costs far more and fails the IRAS test; a pure term policy is the standard choice.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Buying a policy with cash value
An endowment or investment-linked policy is not deductible under the IRAS guide, whatever it is labelled, because the business receives a payout even if nothing happens to the key person. Buy a pure term policy and keep the investment decision separate.
Setting the sum above annual profits
IRAS states that where the capital sum assured exceeds the annual profits of the business, the premiums are not deductible. Document the profit attributable to the person and size the sum from it; use a separate, non-deductible policy for a loan or share buyout.
Assuming a bank-required policy is deductible
A policy assigned to a lender protects the loan, not the profit stream, and the IRAS test looks at purpose and ownership. Treat the premium as non-deductible unless your tax adviser confirms otherwise, and keep the payout tax position clear in the loan planning.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Are keyman insurance premiums tax-deductible in Singapore?

Only if all five IRAS conditions are met: the purpose is to insure against loss of profits from the key person's death or disability; the sum insured relates directly to the annual profits attributable to that person; the policy stays the property of the business with no assignment to the insured or family; it has no cash surrender or investment value; and the loss would not destroy the entire profit-making structure. The guide is in its third edition, published 30 January 2026.

Is the payout taxable?

Where the premiums qualified for deduction, IRAS treats any recovery under the policy as a trading receipt and taxes it. Where the premiums were not deductible, because the policy protects capital rather than profit, the proceeds are capital in nature. Decide the treatment when the policy is bought, not at claim, and record it in the tax computation.

Can a sole proprietor or majority shareholder be the key person?

A sole proprietor is denied a deduction for cover on their own life, because the business is not separate from them. A company whose key person owns a substantial share, alone or with relatives, may also be refused where the facts show the benefit accrues to that person personally. The policy can still be bought; only the deduction is at risk.

How much cover should the company buy?

For a deductible policy, a sum tied to the profit the person generates; IRAS states the premiums are not deductible where the sum exceeds the annual profits of the business. For a loan, the outstanding balance over the facility term. For a buy-sell agreement, the agreed value of the shares. Many companies hold two policies to keep the tax positions separate.

Who owns the policy and who gets paid?

The company applies, pays the premium, owns the policy and receives the proceeds. The key person signs a consent to be insured and completes the medical. If the person leaves, the company can keep the policy, lapse it or, with the insurer's agreement, transfer it to the individual; a transfer is an assignment and ends any deduction from that date.

What must I file with IRAS?

Nothing with the return. The basis for treating the person as a key person must be stated in the tax computation, and the supporting documents, policy, accounts and attribution workings, kept for production on request. A short memo at purchase that sets out role, attributable profit and the sum assured is usually enough.

Sources: IRAS e-Tax Guide: Deductibility of Keyman Insurance Premiums, third edition (PDF), IRAS: tax treatment of business expenses, insurance premiums
