Fidelity guarantee insurance in Singapore: employee theft cover

By the TRS broking team10 Nov 20266 min read
The short answerFidelity guarantee insurance pays an employer for direct financial loss caused by the fraud, forgery or dishonesty of its own employees. It covers cash, stock and goods taken by staff, and is written on a discovery basis: the loss must be found during the policy period or a stated period after it. Theft by outsiders, directors' fraud and losses found too late are the common exclusions.

What fidelity guarantee insurance covers

One Singapore insurer's published wording describes the cover as direct financial loss sustained by reason of any act of forgery, fraud or dishonesty of monies or goods by an employee insured. The policy can cover all employees or designated ones.

The typical loss patterns in Singapore businesses:

  • A finance executive raises payments to a supplier account they control.
  • A cashier voids sales after taking the cash.
  • A storekeeper removes stock and alters the inventory record.
  • A payroll clerk adds a ghost employee.
  • An accounts clerk forges a director's signature on cheques.

Each is a loss of the employer's own money or goods. The policy pays the amount lost, less any excess, up to the limit per employee and in aggregate. It does not pay consequential losses such as lost profit, audit costs beyond what the wording allows, or reputational damage.

Criminal breach of trust by an employee is an offence under Section 408 of the Penal Code, with imprisonment of up to 15 years. Prosecution punishes the employee. It rarely recovers the money. The policy exists for the recovery.

Named employee or blanket cover?

Fidelity guarantee is structured in 3 ways. The choice sets the premium and decides whether an unexpected thief is covered.

StructureHow employees are coveredSuitsGap
Named employeeEach person listed by name with a limitFirms with 2 or 3 staff handling moneyA new hire or unlisted employee is uninsured
PositionRoles listed, such as cashier or accounts executive, whoever holds themFirms with turnover in staff but stable rolesA role not listed is uninsured
BlanketEvery employee, with a limit per employee and in aggregateFirms with 10 or more staff, or where any employee can access money or stockHighest premium of the 3

Blanket cover removes the question of who. Most fraud in small firms is committed by a trusted long-serving employee whom the owner would not have named. SME packages for shops and food outlets often include a fidelity section with a low limit per employee and in aggregate. That limit is small against a fraud that runs for 2 years before discovery. A standalone policy sets the limit against the cash and stock one employee can reach.

How the discovery period works

Fidelity guarantee is written on a discovery basis. The policy that responds is the one in force when the loss is discovered, not when the theft took place. Most wordings then add 2 time limits:

  • A retroactive limit on how far back the acts can go, often 12 or 24 months before discovery.
  • A discovery period after the policy ends or an employee leaves, often 6 to 12 months.

Worked example. An accounts executive diverts S$4,000 a month for 30 months, S$120,000 in total, and resigns. The fraud is found 4 months later during the year-end audit. A policy with a 24-month retroactive limit pays for 24 months of theft, S$96,000, less the excess and capped at the limit. The first 6 months, S$24,000, fall outside it. A discovery period of 6 months after the employee left means the 4-month gap does not defeat the claim. A 3-month discovery period would. The example is arithmetic, not a quotation.

The broker's job at renewal is to keep the retroactive date continuous. A gap between policies, or a change of insurer without carrying prior acts, removes cover for the long-running fraud that is the most expensive kind.

What the insurer asks for at claim time

Fidelity claims are documented claims. One insurer's published claim guide lists the file:

  • Completed claim form.
  • Police report and the police investigation result.
  • Internal investigation result.
  • CCTV footage where it exists.
  • Letter of employment and termination letter.
  • Description of the employee's job duties and the duty roster.
  • Records supporting the amount claimed.
  • Details of any restitution made by the employee.

The police report is not optional. Insurers treat a fidelity loss as a crime, and the report is the evidence that the employer treats it the same way. An employer who agrees a private repayment plan with the employee and does not report may find the claim declined.

The records supporting the amount are where claims stall. Bank statements, ledgers, invoices and stock counts must reconstruct the loss month by month. A firm with no segregation of duties often has no records that show when the loss began, and the retroactive limit then falls at the insurer's reading.

Fidelity guarantee or crime insurance?

Crime insurance, also called commercial crime, is the wider policy. Fidelity guarantee is one section of it.

LossFidelity guaranteeCommercial crime
Employee theft of money or goodsCoveredCovered
Forgery of cheques by an employeeCoveredCovered
Forgery by an outsiderExcludedCovered
Funds transfer fraud, hacked payment instructionsExcludedCovered
Social engineering: a fake supplier email diverts a paymentExcludedCovered where the extension is bought
Theft of money by a burglarExcluded; money insurance respondsCovered under premises section
Collusion between employee and outsiderCoveredCovered

A firm whose main exposure is a payment diverted by a spoofed email has no fidelity claim: no employee was dishonest. That loss sits in crime cover, or in the social engineering extension of a cyber policy. The broker matches the policy to how the money leaves the building.

Controls the underwriter expects

Underwriters price fidelity guarantee on controls, because controls decide how long a fraud runs before it is found. The questions on every proposal form:

  • Segregation of duties: the person who raises a payment does not approve it or reconcile the bank.
  • Dual authorisation on payments above a threshold.
  • Monthly bank reconciliation by someone other than the bookkeeper.
  • Annual audit or review by an external accountant.
  • Stock counts at set intervals, by staff who do not control the stock.
  • Reference checks on staff handling money.

A firm that answers no to the first 3 may be declined or offered a higher excess. The same firm 6 months later, with a second signatory and an outsourced reconciliation, is insurable at normal terms. No premium is quoted here. The limit chosen, the structure, headcount and the controls above are what set it.

Frequently asked questions

Is fraud by a director covered?

Usually not. Fidelity wordings define employee to exclude directors and partners unless they are also salaried employees working under the control of the board. Fraud by a controlling director is excluded outright. Crime insurance with a directors extension, or D&O for the company's own loss, are the alternatives.

Does the policy pay if the theft is discovered after the employee leaves?

Yes, within the discovery period the wording allows after the employee's departure, often 6 to 12 months. A loss found after that period is not covered even if the acts fall inside the retroactive limit. Exit audits for staff who handled money protect the claim.

Is fidelity guarantee the same as crime insurance?

No. Fidelity guarantee covers dishonesty by employees. Crime insurance includes that and adds outsider forgery, funds transfer fraud, premises theft and, by extension, social engineering. A firm exposed to payment diversion by email needs crime or cyber cover, not fidelity alone.

Do I need a police report to claim?

Yes. Published claim requirements list the police report and the investigation result alongside the internal investigation, employment records and the figures supporting the loss. Settling privately with the employee without a report puts the claim at risk.
