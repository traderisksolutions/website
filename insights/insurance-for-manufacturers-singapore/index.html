Insurance for manufacturers in Singapore

By the TRS broking team13 Nov 20265 min read
The short answerA manufacturer in Singapore must insure every manual worker under the Work Injury Compensation Act, and any company vehicle for third-party motor risks. Everything else is commercial choice or a contract requirement. Most factories carry 7 covers: property all risks, machinery breakdown, business interruption, public and product liability, product recall, marine cargo and work injury compensation.

What a manufacturing business in Singapore must insure

Two covers are compulsory by statute for most manufacturers.

Work injury compensation insurance is compulsory for every employee doing manual work, regardless of salary. It is also compulsory for non-manual employees earning S$2,600 a month or less, excluding overtime, bonus, annual wage supplement, incentives and allowances. Other staff are optional to insure, but the employer must still pay valid claims. Failing to insure a required employee carries a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both. Policies must come from an MOM-designated insurer on MOM's compulsory terms.

Motor insurance against third-party bodily injury is compulsory for every vehicle used on a road, including delivery lorries and forklifts driven on public roads.

The third set of obligations comes from contracts, not statute. Landlords of JTC and private industrial premises usually require the tenant to insure its fit-out and carry public liability. Customers, especially multinational buyers, often require product liability limits and a certificate before the first purchase order. Banks financing equipment require the asset to be insured with the bank noted as loss payee.

Manufacturers of consumer goods face a further regulator. The Consumer Product Safety Office regulates controlled goods, which must be registered before sale in Singapore.

Property all risks does not cover machinery breakdown. A property policy covers external causes such as fire or flood. A motor burning out from an internal fault needs a machinery breakdown policy. The two policies should be arranged together so the boundary between them has no gap.

Risks specific to manufacturing

Fire is the loss that closes factories. Property insurance claims across the Singapore market rose 60.5% in 2025, while property premiums grew 4.1% to S$864.1 million. The General Insurance Association attributes the claims rise to more fire incidents in residential and commercial property and several large losses.

Product liability is the second exposure. The Sale of Goods Act 1979 implies terms of satisfactory quality and fitness for purpose into every contract of sale. A buyer whose goods fail can claim against the seller in contract. The seller then seeks recovery up the chain from the component maker. Whoever sold the goods carries the first claim.

Four other risks recur in manufacturing:

  • Single-line dependency. One extruder, kiln or moulding press can stop all output when it fails.
  • Underinsured stock. Raw material prices move, and a sum insured set a year ago can fall short. Section 81 of the Marine Insurance Act 1906 states the average principle: an assured insured below value carries the uninsured balance. Property policies apply the same rule.
  • Export exposure. Products sold overseas may face claims under foreign law, which needs worldwide jurisdiction on the liability policy.
  • Hot work and dust. Welding, cutting and combustible dust are frequent fire causes that insurers survey.

What the broker needs from you

A complete submission shortens quotation time and avoids non-disclosure arguments at claim time. Prepare these 10 items:

  1. Company profile, UEN and a description of every process carried out on site.
  2. Site address, building construction, age and lease terms.
  3. Fire protection details: sprinklers, alarms, hose reels and the date of the last service.
  4. Sum insured for building fit-out, plant, stock and finished goods, with the valuation basis.
  5. List of key machines, their age, replacement value and maintenance contracts.
  6. Annual turnover split by domestic and export sales, with export countries named.
  7. Product list, end uses and quality certifications held.
  8. Gross profit figures for the last financial year, for business interruption.
  9. Headcount and wage roll split by manual and non-manual staff.
  10. Claims history for the past 5 years across every policy.

Supply contracts with customers are also useful. They show the limits and indemnities the business has already agreed to provide.

Frequently asked questions

Does property all risks cover machinery breakdown?

No. Property all risks covers damage from external causes such as fire, flood and impact. Internal mechanical or electrical failure is excluded and needs a machinery breakdown policy. Arrange both together so the boundary between them is covered.

Who is liable for a defective component: maker or assembler?

The seller to the injured buyer is liable first, under the implied terms of the Sale of Goods Act 1979. The assembler then seeks recovery from the component maker under their own supply contract. Each party needs product liability cover and a clear indemnity clause in its supply terms.

What is business interruption for a factory with one production line?

It pays the gross profit lost while the line is rebuilt after insured damage. A single-line factory should consider the lead time for replacement plant when choosing the indemnity period. Business interruption following machinery breakdown needs its own cover, linked to the breakdown policy.

Are export products covered for overseas claims?

Only if the liability policy's territory and jurisdiction include the export countries. Many policies exclude claims brought in the United States and Canada unless added. Declare export sales by country so the insurer can rate them.
