D&O insurance for SMEs in Singapore: cost, limits and retentions

By the TRS broking team10 Nov 20266 min read
The short answerD&amp;O insurance for SMEs in Singapore is priced on turnover, balance sheet, ownership and sector, not on headcount. It is not required by law. Section 172 of the Companies Act permits a company to buy it, and investors and lenders write it into term sheets. Limits for private companies are usually S$1 million to S$5 million, chosen against the claims a director can face.

What D&O insurance covers

Directors and officers liability insurance pays the personal liability of a director, officer or senior manager for a wrongful act in managing the company. A wrongful act is a breach of duty, error, misstatement or omission in that role. The policy pays defence costs, investigation costs and damages or settlements, up to the limit.

Claims against private company directors come from 5 directions:

  • Shareholders, including minority investors, over dilution, related-party deals or misstatements.
  • Liquidators and creditors after insolvency, for wrongful trading or preferential payments.
  • Regulators: ACRA, MAS, the PDPC, MOM and IRAS, for investigations and penalties where insurable.
  • Employees, for wrongful dismissal, discrimination and harassment claims against individuals.
  • Competitors and counterparties, for inducing breach of contract or misrepresentation.

The company itself is covered only where the policy includes entity cover, discussed below. See D&O insurance for private companies for the duties that give rise to claims.

Side A, Side B and Side C cover

A D&O policy is written in 3 insuring clauses. The letters describe who is paid.

ClauseWho is paidWhen it respondsRetention
Side AThe director personallyCompany cannot or will not indemnify (insolvency, legal prohibition)Usually nil
Side BThe companyCompany has indemnified the director and claims reimbursementYes, paid by the company
Side CThe company for its own liabilitySecurities claims (listed) or entity claims where extended (private)Yes, often higher

Side A matters most at the point of insolvency. A liquidator sues the directors, the company has no money to indemnify them, and Side A pays their defence. Side B is the common route for a solvent company. Side C, entity cover, is where private company policies differ most. Some wordings extend entity cover to employment claims and regulatory investigations against the company. Others limit it to the directors.

One shared limit covers all 3 sides. A large entity claim under Side C can leave nothing for the directors under Side A. The broker checks whether a separate Side A limit or an order-of-payments clause is available.

Why the insolvency numbers matter

Compulsory liquidations in Singapore rose from 201 in 2023 to 307 in 2024 and 392 in 2025. Each liquidation puts a liquidator in charge with a duty to pursue recoveries. Claims against former directors for wrongful trading, breach of duty or undervalue transactions follow.

A director of a failed company faces those claims personally, years after the event. A D&O policy in force when the claim is made, with a retroactive date that predates the conduct, is the only cover. The policy is claims-made, so letting it lapse at the first sign of trouble removes the protection when it is needed. Run-off cover for 6 years after a sale or closure is the standard answer, matching the limitation period for most civil claims.

How much D&O limit does a private company buy?

There is no statutory limit. The broker benchmarks against 4 figures:

  • Investor or lender requirement in the shareholders' agreement or facility letter.
  • Largest realistic single claim: a shareholder dispute at the value of the disputed stake, or a liquidator claim at the value of the debts incurred while trading.
  • Regulatory exposure. The Personal Data Protection Act allows financial penalties up to S$1 million, or 10% of Singapore turnover for organisations with turnover above S$10 million. Whether a penalty is insurable depends on the wording and the law, but the investigation costs are.
  • Defence costs, which erode the limit before any damages are paid. A contested High Court action can run into 7 figures.

A company with S$5 million to S$20 million turnover typically lands between S$1 million and S$5 million. A S$1 million limit covers defence of one serious matter. S$5 million covers a contested dispute with damages. The limit should rise at each funding round, because each round adds shareholders with standing to sue. One regional broker reports placing more than US$100 million of D&O premium a year and running limit benchmarking by sector.

How a retention works

The retention, also called the deductible, is the first part of each Side B or Side C claim that the company pays. Side A usually carries none, because the director should not pay out of pocket when the company cannot.

Worked example. A former employee sues 2 directors and the company for wrongful dismissal. Defence costs reach S$180,000 and the matter settles for S$120,000, a total of S$300,000. With a S$25,000 retention under Side B, the company pays S$25,000 and the insurer pays S$275,000. Had the company been insolvent, Side A would have paid the directors' defence from the first dollar. The figures are illustrative arithmetic, not a quotation.

Retention choices move the premium. A higher retention on entity claims, which are more frequent, lowers the price without weakening Side A.

What moves the D&O premium

No premium is quoted here, and the broker does not promise one. Underwriters price an SME D&O programme on:

  • Turnover and total assets, which set the rating base.
  • Financial health: 2 years of audited accounts, cash runway, debt covenants.
  • Ownership: founder-held, private equity, venture, family. Outside shareholders raise the risk.
  • Sector: financial services, crypto, construction and healthcare rate higher.
  • Overseas operations, with US exposure rated highest.
  • Claims and investigation history, and any director previously disqualified.

Limit and retention then scale the result. A business with clean audits, no outside investors and no US sales sits at the bottom of the range. The same business after a Series A with US customers sits well above it. Premium ranges quoted on advisory websites are not insurer rates and are left out here.

Frequently asked questions

How much D&O limit does a S$5-20 million turnover company buy?

Commonly S$1 million to S$5 million, set against the investor requirement, the largest realistic claim and defence costs. The limit is shared across all directors and all claims in the year, so a single large matter can exhaust a S$1 million policy.

Does D&O cover the company itself or only directors?

Directors and officers are the core insureds. The company is covered under Side B for amounts it reimburses them, and under Side C only where entity cover is included. Private company wordings vary on how far Side C extends, so the clause must be read.

What is a retention and who pays it?

The retention is the first amount of a Side B or Side C claim, paid by the company. Side A, which pays a director directly when the company cannot indemnify, usually has no retention. Raising the entity retention lowers the premium without exposing directors.

Does D&O respond to an insolvency claim against directors?

Yes, where the policy is in force when the claim is made and the conduct postdates the retroactive date. Side A pays defence costs when the insolvent company cannot. Policies often carry an insolvency exclusion for claims by the company itself, so the wording should be checked before a company enters difficulty.
