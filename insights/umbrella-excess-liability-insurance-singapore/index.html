Umbrella and excess liability insurance in Singapore

By the TRS broking team13 Nov 20265 min read
The short answerExcess liability insurance adds a higher limit above a primary liability policy and follows its terms. Umbrella liability insurance adds limit above several primary policies at once, such as public liability, motor and employer's liability, and may cover some claims the primary policies exclude. Both pay only after the primary limit is used up. Businesses buy them when a contract or exposure needs more limit than one policy carries.

What excess and umbrella liability mean

Primary policy. The first policy to respond to a claim, such as public liability with a S$2 million limit. It handles the claim from the first dollar above the excess.

Excess liability. A policy that sits above one primary policy and pays once the primary limit is exhausted. Most excess policies are "follow form": they adopt the primary policy's terms, exclusions and conditions. They add limit, not cover.

Umbrella liability. A policy that sits above several primary policies at once. It adds limit over each one. Some umbrella wordings also "drop down" to cover a claim the primary policy excludes, after a retention the business pays itself.

Layer. Each policy in a tower is described by its limit and its attachment point. "S$3 million excess of S$2 million" means the policy pays from S$2 million up to S$5 million.

Tower. The full stack of primary and excess layers, often placed with different insurers.

The difference in one line: excess liability stretches one policy upward; umbrella stretches several and may widen cover.

Worked example: an S$8 million claim

A contractor carries the following programme. The figures are an example only.

LayerLimitAttaches atPays
Primary public liabilityS$2,000,000Above the S$10,000 excessS$2,000,000
First excess layerS$3,000,000S$2,000,000S$3,000,000
Second excess layerS$5,000,000S$5,000,000S$3,000,000
Total towerS$10,000,000S$8,000,000

A crane failure injures 3 members of the public and damages a neighbouring building. Agreed damages and costs total S$8,010,000.

  1. The contractor pays the S$10,000 excess.
  2. The primary insurer pays its full S$2,000,000 limit.
  3. The first excess insurer pays its full S$3,000,000.
  4. The second excess insurer pays S$3,000,000, leaving S$2,000,000 of its limit unused.

Had the tower stopped at S$5 million, the contractor would have paid a further S$3,000,000 itself. Each layer pays only after the one below is exhausted. If the primary policy excludes the claim, follow-form excess layers exclude it too.

When a business needs more limit

Four triggers drive businesses beyond a single primary limit.

  1. Contract requirements. Clients and main contractors set minimum limits. Government construction contracts on the Public Sector Standard Conditions of Contract carry insurance obligations for third-party liability, works and work injury compensation. The current construction works edition is the 8th, from July 2020, with a PSSCOC-lite edition issued in March 2025.
  2. Severity of exposure. Work near public roads, crowds, cranes or hazardous materials can produce claims above S$5 million.
  3. Long-tail claims. The Limitation Act 1959 gives 6 years to sue in contract or tort from when the cause of action accrues, longer for some latent damage. A claim arising years later is paid at future award levels.
  4. Several policies with low limits. Motor, public and product liability each carry separate limits. An umbrella raises all of them at once. How public and product liability split is set out in public liability vs product liability.

Third-party motor cover is compulsory for every vehicle on a road under the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act 1960. Fleet operators often place an umbrella above their motor policies for property damage and bodily injury exposures beyond the primary limit.

Excess liability is the simpler choice when one contract asks for a higher public liability limit. Umbrella fits a business with several liability policies and a single total limit to maintain.

Umbrella policies usually do not sit above work injury compensation benefits under the Work Injury Compensation Act. Those are statutory amounts, paid by the WIC policy. An umbrella may sit above the employer's liability section covering common law claims, if the wording schedules it.

Common mistakes

  1. Gaps between layers. An excess layer attaching at S$3 million above a primary of S$2 million leaves a S$1 million gap the business pays.
  2. Mismatched terms. An excess layer with its own exclusions can refuse a claim the primary paid.
  3. Aggregate erosion. A primary aggregate used by earlier claims leaves the excess layer exposed or the gap uninsured, depending on drop-down wording.
  4. Different renewal dates. Layers expiring at different times can leave part of the tower lapsed.
  5. Ignoring the underlying schedule. An umbrella requires each underlying policy to keep its stated limit. Reducing one without telling the umbrella insurer creates a gap.

Insurance brokers in Singapore must be registered by MAS under the Insurance Act 1966. A registered broker places each layer and checks that the attachment points join without gaps.

Frequently asked questions

When does a Singapore SME need more than S$5 million of liability cover?

When a contract requires it, or when one incident could injure several people or damage high-value property. Contractors, event organisers and fleet operators meet these conditions most often. The limit should match the worst credible single event, not average claims.

Does an umbrella sit over WICA?

Not over the statutory compensation itself. WIC policies pay the amounts set by MOM. An umbrella may sit above the common law employer's liability section, if the umbrella wording schedules that section as underlying.

Is excess liability cheaper per million than the primary layer?

Usually yes. The primary layer pays every claim from the first dollar above the excess, while higher layers pay only rare large claims. Each higher layer generally costs less per million of limit than the one below. Exact pricing depends on the exposure and market conditions.

Can a client's S$10 million requirement be met with layers?

Usually, if the contract permits it. A primary policy plus one or more excess layers can total S$10 million. Confirm the contract accepts a layered programme and that the certificate shows each layer's limit and attachment point.
