Insurance for retail shops in Singapore

By the TRS broking team13 Nov 20265 min read
The short answerA retail shop in Singapore must insure its employees under WICA. Its other covers come from the lease and from what the shop holds: stock, fit-out, shopfront glass, cash and footfall. A published shop package costs from S$294.30 a year for S$100,000 of contents and stock. Stock must be insured at its full value, or claims are reduced in proportion.

What a retail business in Singapore must insure

WICA is the one cover the law requires of a shop. The Ministry of Manpower sets who must be insured.

  • Every employee doing manual work, whatever the salary. Stockroom staff, delivery staff and cleaners count as manual.
  • Every non-manual employee earning S$2,600 a month or less. The figure excludes overtime, bonus, annual wage supplement, incentives and allowances.
  • Non-manual staff earning more are optional to insure. The employer must still pay any valid claim.
  • Failing to insure a required employee carries a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both.

Part-time and temporary shop staff are employees. The same salary test applies to their monthly earnings.

Mall and shophouse leases add contractual requirements. Most require the tenant to insure fit-out, plate glass and public liability. Many require the landlord to be named on the policy and given notice of cancellation.

Risks specific to retail

Stock value swings. Stock peaks before festive seasons and drops after them. A sum insured set in a quiet month can be short at peak. Section 81 of the Marine Insurance Act 1906 sets out the average principle: an assured insured below full value bears the uninsured balance. Property policies apply it through an average clause. For example, a shop insuring S$200,000 of stock valued at S$400,000 recovers half of any loss.

Theft. Shoplifting, break-ins and staff theft are the most frequent retail losses. Many all risks wordings limit or exclude theft without forcible entry. The shop should read the theft clause before relying on it.

Shopfront glass. Glass is fragile and exposed to the public. Liberty's ShopCare covers accidental breakage of plate glass up to S$25,000 per loss and in aggregate.

Cash handling. Tills and safes hold cash overnight, and staff carry takings to the bank. Money cover pays for theft from the premises and in transit, within set limits for each location.

Customer injury. Slips on wet floors and falling stock are the common public liability claims in shops.

What moves the premium

Seven factors move a shop's premium.

  • Stock value at peak. The largest property driver. Liberty's published all risks top-up for shops is S$109 per S$50,000, against S$68.13 for offices.
  • Type of goods. Electronics, phones and luxury goods attract theft. Packages exclude jewellers, pawnshops and tobacco, wine or spirits retailers.
  • Security. Shutters, alarms, CCTV and safes reduce the theft rate.
  • Location. Road-level and basement units carry a S$2,500 flood excess on the published package.
  • Cash held. Higher money limits cost more. Liberty publishes S$5.45 per extra S$500 of money cover.
  • Public liability limit. Set by the lease. The published top-up is S$54.50 per extra S$500,000.
  • Claims history. Liberty's OfficeCare package gives a 10% no-claim discount at first renewal and 15% after two claim-free years.

Property claims in Singapore rose 60.5% in 2025, according to the General Insurance Association of Singapore. Premiums rose 4.1%. That gap pushes property rates up for shops with recent losses.

Shops above package limits are placed on the commercial property form, with separate liability and WICA policies.

What the broker needs from you

A broker places shop insurance from these documents and figures.

  1. The lease's insurance clause, with required limits, glass cover and named parties.
  2. Fit-out cost and completion date for each outlet.
  3. Stock value at cost for each outlet, at average and at peak.
  4. A description of goods sold, with the share of high-value items.
  5. Security details: shutters, alarms, CCTV and safe rating.
  6. Cash held overnight and the largest amount carried to the bank.
  7. Headcount by role, including part-time staff, with annual wages.
  8. Claims for recent policy years, with dates, causes and amounts.

For a chain, the broker places all outlets on one policy with a schedule of locations. One policy simplifies renewals and gives a single claims record.

Frequently asked questions

Is stock insured at cost or selling price?

The policy states the basis. Liberty's ShopCare insures stock-in-trade at market value, without allowance for wear and tear. Many larger policies insure stock at cost, so the shop should check the wording before setting values.

Does shop insurance cover theft without forced entry?

Often not, or only within a sub-limit. Many all risks wordings require forcible and violent entry for a theft claim. Shoplifting and staff theft usually need specific cover, such as fidelity guarantee for employee theft.

What does the mall lease require?

The lease sets the covers, limits and named parties. Mall leases commonly require public liability, plate glass and fit-out cover, with the landlord named. The broker reads the clause and matches each requirement to a policy.

Are part-time retail staff covered under WICA?

Part-time staff are employees under WICA. Manual staff must be insured at any salary, and non-manual staff earning S$2,600 a month or less. Liberty's package classes cashiers and sales staff as non-manual.
