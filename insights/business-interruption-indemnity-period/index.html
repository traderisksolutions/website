Business interruption insurance: indemnity period, gross profit and what gets paid

By the TRS broking team24 Oct 20266 min read
The short answerBusiness interruption pays the gross profit a business loses, and the extra costs it incurs, after insured damage to its property stops or slows trading. It pays for the indemnity period chosen on the policy, so that period must cover the full time needed to recover, not just to rebuild.

What triggers a claim?

Business interruption normally follows insured physical damage, such as a fire, under the property policy. Without covered damage, there is usually no BI claim.

How is the indemnity period set?

It is the time from the damage until the business is trading as before. Rebuilding, re-equipping and winning back customers all take time; 12 months is often too short for manufacturers.

What is the average clause?

If the gross profit insured is below the actual figure, a claim can be reduced in proportion. Recalculate the sum insured every year.

Frequently asked questions

Does BI cover a pandemic closure?

Most policies exclude closures without physical damage, including disease outbreaks, unless a specific extension applies.
