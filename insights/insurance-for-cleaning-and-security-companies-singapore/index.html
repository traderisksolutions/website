Insurance for cleaning and security companies in Singapore

By the TRS broking team13 Nov 20265 min read
The short answerA cleaning or security company in Singapore must insure every manual worker under the Work Injury Compensation Act, whatever the salary. Cleaners and security officers are manual workers, so the whole frontline workforce needs work injury compensation insurance. Beyond that, most service contracts require public liability, and many require fidelity guarantee cover against staff dishonesty on client premises.

What a cleaning or security business in Singapore must insure

Work injury compensation insurance is compulsory for every employee doing manual work, regardless of salary. It is also compulsory for non-manual employees earning S$2,600 a month or less, excluding overtime, bonus, AWS, incentives and allowances. Supervisors and office staff above that threshold are optional to insure, but valid claims must still be paid.

Failing to insure a required employee carries a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both. Since 1 January 2021, policies must come from an MOM-designated insurer on MOM's compulsory terms.

Both sectors are also licensed, and licence conditions shape the business.

  • Cleaning. Cleaning companies hold a Cleaning Business Licence administered by the National Environment Agency. They must meet the Progressive Wage Model wage and training requirements to obtain or renew it. From 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, the basic monthly wage for a general or indoor cleaner at office and commercial sites is S$2,080.
  • Security. Security agencies and security officers must be licensed under the Private Security Industry Act 2007.

Wage floors matter for insurance because the declared wage roll drives the work injury compensation premium. A wage roll that has not been updated for Progressive Wage Model increases understates the risk.

Public liability is the cover clients check first. It pays for injury to a third party or damage to client property caused by the business's work. A wet floor that causes a fall, or a chemical that damages marble, sits here.

Risks specific to cleaning and security

Both trades work on premises they do not control. That shapes every claim.

  1. Slips, falls and chemical injuries. Cleaners carry high frequency of minor injuries. Each one can trigger medical leave wages under WICA.
  2. Work at height. Facade and high-level cleaning raises the severity of falls.
  3. Third-party injury. Members of the public slip on wet floors or trip over equipment.
  4. Damage to client property. Wrong chemicals on stone, water on electrical rooms, or a dropped key that forces re-keying.
  5. Staff dishonesty. Cleaners and guards have access to offices, homes and stores after hours.
  6. Assault and confrontation. Security officers face injury from intruders or members of the public.

WICA amounts rose for accidents from 1 November 2025. Medical expenses are now covered up to S$53,000 or 1 year. Permanent incapacity compensation ranges from S$116,000 to S$346,000, multiplied by the percentage of incapacity. Death compensation ranges from S$91,000 to S$269,000. For a workforce in the hundreds, these limits set the size of the exposure.

Contract terms also move price. A client requiring joint names or a waiver of subrogation asks the insurer to give up recovery rights, which some insurers charge for.

What the broker needs from you

A full submission avoids underinsurance and disputes at claim time. Prepare the following:

  1. Company profile, UEN and the NEA or police licence numbers.
  2. Headcount split by cleaners, security officers, supervisors and office staff.
  3. Annual wage roll for each group, updated for current Progressive Wage Model rates.
  4. Number of Work Permit, S Pass and local employees.
  5. List of client sites by type, and any facade or height work.
  6. Insurance requirements from the 5 largest service contracts.
  7. Staff vetting and induction procedures.
  8. Vehicle list for the motor policy.
  9. Equipment list with replacement values.
  10. Claims history for the past 5 years on every policy.

Contract requirements usually set the public liability limit and fidelity limit. Send them to the broker before the quotation, not after.

Frequently asked questions

What public liability limit do building owners require of cleaning contractors?

There is no statutory figure. The limit comes from each service contract, and building owners and managing agents set their own minimums. Collect the requirements from all current contracts and buy a limit that meets the highest one.

Does WICA cover part-time cleaners on hourly pay?

Cleaners are manual workers, and WICA insurance is compulsory for every employee doing manual work regardless of salary. Part-time employees are employees, so they must be declared and insured. Include their wages in the declared wage roll.

Is theft by a security officer covered?

Not by public liability, which excludes dishonest acts. Fidelity guarantee insurance covers loss from theft or fraud by employees. Check whether the policy extends to client property and whether it requires proof of the specific employee responsible.

Who insures when staff are deployed to a client site?

The employer insures its own staff under WICA, wherever they are deployed. The employer's public liability covers damage or injury its staff cause at the site. The client insures its own building and contents under its property policy.
