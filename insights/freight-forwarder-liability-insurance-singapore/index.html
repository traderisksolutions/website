Freight forwarder liability insurance in Singapore explained

By the TRS broking team10 Nov 20266 min read
The short answerFreight forwarder liability insurance in Singapore covers what a forwarder is legally liable to pay when cargo is lost, damaged or delayed through its error. It is not cargo insurance. Cargo insurance pays the owner the value of the goods. Liability insurance pays the forwarder's legal liability, which is often capped by contract or statute at a fraction of that value.

Liability insurance or cargo insurance?

The 2 policies answer different questions. Cargo insurance asks: what is the value of the goods? Liability insurance asks: what does the forwarder owe?

Who is insuredOwner of the goodsThe forwarder or logistics operator
What is paidAgreed or market value of the goodsLegal liability, capped by contract or convention
TriggerPhysical loss or damage in transitNegligence, error or omission by the forwarder
Fault neededNoYes
Typical limitFull shipment valuePer package, per kilogram or per event

Under Incoterms 2020, only CIF and CIP oblige a party to buy cargo insurance. In every other term the forwarder is not the insuring party. The shipper decides whether to insure the goods. The forwarder insures its own liability, and nothing more.

A shipper who did not insure will still claim against the forwarder. The liability policy defends that claim and pays what the law requires. See marine cargo and the Institute Cargo Clauses for the cargo side.

Who buys logistics liability insurance?

Insurers write this class for a defined list of operators:

  • Freight forwarders acting as agent or as contracting carrier.
  • Non-vessel operating common carriers (NVOCCs) that issue their own bills of lading or air waybills.
  • Warehousemen and consolidators holding goods for onward transit.
  • Haulage companies moving containers between port, depot and customer.
  • Customs brokers clearing goods for importers and exporters.
  • Multimodal transport operators that take responsibility door to door.

One insurer's published appetite is for warehousing incidental to transit, not stand-alone warehousing. A business whose main revenue is storage needs warehouse keepers' liability instead. The broker checks the revenue split before placing the risk, because an operator that outgrows the appetite loses cover for the storage leg.

What the policy covers

A forwarder's liability policy usually runs to 4 sections.

  • Cargo liability: loss of or damage to goods in the forwarder's care, custody or control.
  • Errors and omissions: financial loss to a customer from a mistake that did not damage the goods. Examples are a missed sailing, wrong documentation or goods released to the wrong party.
  • Customs fines and duties: penalties imposed on the forwarder, or on a customer, for errors in declarations the forwarder prepared.
  • Third-party liability: injury or property damage to others arising from operations.

Defence costs sit on top of these. Costs of disposing of damaged cargo, quarantine and survey fees are usually included or available by extension.

Errors and omissions is the section most often undervalued. A forwarder that fails to file an export permit on time can cost its customer a contract. No cargo was touched, so cargo liability does not respond. Only the E&O section does.

How standard trading conditions and Hague-Visby cap the exposure

A forwarder's liability is limited twice. First by its own standard trading conditions. Second by the conventions written into Singapore law.

The Carriage of Goods by Sea Act applies the Hague-Visby Rules to bills of lading. The carrier's liability is capped at 666.67 units of account per package or 2 units of account per kilogram, whichever is higher. The unit of account is the IMF Special Drawing Right (SDR).

Worked example. A 1,000 kg consignment packed as 4 pallets is lost. The per-package limit is 4 × 666.67 = 2,666.68 SDR. The per-kilogram limit is 1,000 × 2 = 2,000 SDR. The higher figure, 2,666.68 SDR, is the carrier's cap. If the goods were valued at S$80,000, the shipper's cargo policy pays the rest or the shipper bears it.

The policy is priced on the forwarder's trading conditions. If the forwarder issues documents without incorporating its conditions, or accepts a customer's terms that remove the limits, liability becomes unlimited. Most policies then either exclude the claim or cap it at the convention figure. The broker reviews the conditions and the signature process before binding.

Warehousing and the operations gap

Goods held in the forwarder's warehouse are covered while storage is incidental to transit. The period insurers accept varies by wording, and the broker confirms it in days.

Stand-alone storage, pick-and-pack and fulfilment are a different trade. They need warehouse keepers' liability, and the warehouse itself needs property cover. Non-residential premises fires rose 13.5% to 471 in Singapore in 2025, and electrical faults caused 43.5% of them. A fire in a bonded warehouse creates a property loss for the owner and a liability claim from every depositor at once.

The Transportation and Storage sector recorded 64 workplace fatal and major injuries in 2025. Its rate rose to 23.8 per 100,000 workers from 18.4 the year before. Work injury compensation for the forwarder's own staff is a separate statutory policy, covered in how WICA premiums are worked out.

What moves the premium

The broker's submission to an insurer sets the price through 6 inputs:

  • Gross freight revenue, split by sea, air, road and warehousing.
  • Whether the forwarder acts as agent or as contracting carrier issuing its own documents.
  • Trading conditions used, and the proportion of customers on their own terms.
  • Cargo types handled, with high-value electronics, pharmaceuticals and temperature-controlled goods rated higher.
  • Claims history for 5 years, including customs penalties.
  • Limits and deductibles chosen for each section.

A forwarder with written conditions on every quotation, a clean customs record and a defined warehouse dwell time presents a tighter risk than one with the same revenue and none of those controls. The broker's submission documents each control, because the underwriter prices what is evidenced, not what is said.

Frequently asked questions

Does my liability policy pay the shipper the full cargo value?

No. It pays the forwarder's legal liability, which is capped by the trading conditions or by convention limits such as Hague-Visby. The shipper's own cargo insurance covers the difference. Some policies offer a higher cargo liability limit for a declared-value surcharge.

What are errors and omissions for a forwarder?

A mistake that causes a customer financial loss without damaging the goods. Examples are missed deadlines, wrong consignee details, incorrect documentation or releasing goods without the original bill of lading. The E&O section pays the customer's proven loss up to the limit.

Are customs penalties insurable?

Yes, where they arise from a negligent error in a declaration and the policy carries a customs fines section. Fines for deliberate misdeclaration or smuggling are not insurable. Insurers also require the forwarder to hold the correct customs licences.

Do I need separate cover for goods held in my warehouse?

If storage is incidental to transit and within the policy's stated period, no. If the business offers storage as a service, or goods sit beyond that period, warehouse keepers' liability is needed. The warehouse building and racking need property insurance in either case.
