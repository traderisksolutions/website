WICA vs group personal accident insurance in Singapore

By the TRS broking team13 Nov 20265 min read
The short answerWICA insurance and group personal accident (GPA) insurance answer different questions. WICA insurance covers the employer's legal duty to compensate employees injured at work, at amounts set by the Ministry of Manpower. GPA pays fixed benefits to employees injured by accident, at work or outside it. WICA is compulsory for most staff. GPA is optional and cannot replace it.

The key point is the trigger. WICA responds to a work injury, whatever the cause. GPA responds to an accident, wherever it happens.

What WICA covers

The Work Injury Compensation Act makes the employer liable to compensate an employee injured by an accident arising out of and in the course of employment. WICA insurance funds that liability.

Insurance is compulsory for every employee doing manual work, regardless of salary. It is also compulsory for non-manual employees earning S$2,600 a month or less, excluding overtime, bonus, AWS, incentives and allowances. Failing to insure carries a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both. Since 1 January 2021, policies must come from an MOM-designated insurer.

For accidents from 1 November 2025, WICA pays:

  • Medical expenses up to S$53,000, or until 1 year from the accident, whichever comes first.
  • Permanent incapacity from S$116,000 to S$346,000, multiplied by the percentage of incapacity.
  • Death from S$91,000 to S$269,000.
  • Medical leave wages at full average monthly earnings for up to 60 days of hospitalisation leave and 14 days of outpatient leave, then two-thirds up to 1 year.

Commuting is covered only in company transport. Travel for work purposes is covered if there is no personal detour.

What group personal accident covers

GPA is a scheduled-benefit policy. The employer chooses a sum insured for each employee or class, often a multiple of annual salary or a fixed amount. The policy pays a set percentage of that sum for each listed injury.

A typical GPA wording includes:

  1. Accidental death.
  2. Permanent total and partial disablement, paid against a scale of injuries.
  3. Medical expenses following an accident, up to a sub-limit.
  4. Weekly benefit for temporary disablement, on some policies.
  5. Extensions such as repatriation or funeral costs.

Most GPA policies cover employees 24 hours a day, worldwide. They pay regardless of fault and regardless of whether the injury was work-related. The insurer pays the benefit to the employee or the estate.

GPA is not a liability policy. It does not respond to the employer's duty under the Act. A GPA payment does not discharge the employer's WICA obligation, and a GPA policy does not meet the compulsory insurance requirement. GPA also excludes illness, which leaves occupational disease to WICA.

Where they overlap and where they leave gaps

The two policies overlap on one event: an accident at work. Both may pay. WICA pays the statutory compensation, and GPA pays its scheduled benefit as well, unless the GPA wording offsets one against the other. Read the GPA wording for any clause that reduces benefits by WICA payments.

The last row is the hidden gap. An employer may choose not to insure non-manual staff earning above S$2,600. It must still pay valid WICA claims for them. A GPA policy does not fund that liability; a WICA policy extended to all staff does.

Which one a business needs

Most businesses need WICA for compulsory staff and decide on GPA as a benefit. Three scenarios show the choice.

Scenario 1: a construction subcontractor with 40 Work Permit holders. WICA is compulsory for all 40. GPA is optional and adds cover off-site and outside working hours. The WICA product page sets out the declaration basis.

Scenario 2: a 15-person software firm, all earning above S$2,600. WICA is not compulsory for any of them. The employer still owes compensation for valid work injury claims. Extending WICA to all staff funds that liability. GPA can be added as a staff benefit for accidents outside work.

Scenario 3: a logistics firm with drivers and office staff. Drivers are manual workers and must be insured under WICA. Office staff earning S$2,600 or less must also be insured. GPA closes the gap for drivers travelling home on public transport, which WICA excludes.

Employer's liability (WIC) premiums across the Singapore market were S$488.2 million in 2025, with an underwriting profit of S$94.4 million, up from S$58.6 million in 2024.

Frequently asked questions

Can a GPA policy satisfy the WICA requirement?

No. The Act requires a work injury compensation policy from an MOM-designated insurer on MOM's compulsory terms. A GPA policy is a different contract with scheduled benefits. Holding GPA alone leaves the employer uninsured for WICA purposes and open to a fine of up to S$10,000.

Does WICA cover an accident on the way to work?

Only when the employee travels in company transport that is not public transport. An accident in the employee's own car, a friend's car or public transport between home and work is not covered. Travel during work for a work purpose is covered if there is no personal detour.

Why buy GPA if WICA is compulsory?

GPA covers accidents that WICA excludes: weekends, holidays and commuting by public transport or private car. It also pays fixed sums quickly without assessing liability. Employers use it as a staff benefit and to cover employees outside working hours.

Does GPA pay on top of WICA for the same accident?

Usually yes, unless the GPA wording reduces its benefit by amounts paid under WICA. Both policies respond to a work accident under their own terms. Check the GPA wording for any offset clause before relying on both payments.
