The cover

Travel insurance in Singapore

Travel insurance pays overseas medical and evacuation costs, refunds non-refundable bookings when a trip is cancelled or cut short for an insured reason, and pays fixed sums for flight delay, baggage delay and lost belongings. Singapore residents buy it per trip or on an annual plan because MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plans do not pay for treatment abroad, and because the Ministry of Foreign Affairs does not fund medical, legal or travel costs for citizens overseas. Some destinations list insurance as an entry condition.

It is not a substitute for health insurance at home and does not cover pre-existing conditions unless a specific plan is bought. Cover is chosen by region, trip length, up to 90 or 180 days a trip, and plan tier, which sets the medical limit, from S$200,000 to unlimited, and the cancellation limit. Cancellation cover starts on purchase; most other sections start on departure. TRS compares delay thresholds, sports exclusions and the pre-existing wording across insurers and files the claim.

Who buys it

  • Families on school-holiday trips with children added at no or low cost.
  • Frequent travellers taking 3 or more trips a year, where an annual plan costs less.
  • Travellers with a diagnosed condition who need the pre-existing plan.
  • Skiers, divers and trekkers checking that the activity is covered.
  • Travellers who paid non-refundable deposits months ahead and want cancellation cover from today.
  • Residents aged 70 and above, where limits and premiums change.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Destination countries and travel dates
  • Names, dates of birth and NRIC or passport numbers
  • Single trip or annual plan
  • Planned activities such as skiing, diving or trekking
  • Pre-existing medical conditions to be covered
  • Non-refundable bookings already paid and their dates

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Region
ASEAN, Asia and worldwide bands price up in that order.
Trip length
Premium rises with days away; a single trip is capped at 90 or 180 days depending on the plan.
Plan tier
Medical limits from S$200,000 to unlimited and cancellation limits from S$5,000 to S$15,000 set the tier price.
Age
Travellers aged 70 and above pay more and get lower limits on most plans.
Pre-existing cover
A plan that covers stable pre-existing conditions costs more and carries a 25% co-payment on some sections.
Add-ons
Cancel-for-any-reason, rental car excess, sports equipment and home contents while away are priced riders.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Buying at the airport
Cancellation cover starts on the policy date, so a policy bought on departure day protects none of the deposits paid earlier. Buy when the first non-refundable booking is made.
Ignoring the look-back period
Insurers exclude any condition treated or showing symptoms in the 12 months before the trip unless a pre-existing plan is bought. Declare conditions and buy the right plan rather than hope the clause is not applied.
Missing the delay threshold
Delay benefits start only after a set number of consecutive hours, 3 or 6 depending on the insurer, and need the carrier's written confirmation. Get the delay letter at the counter before leaving the airport.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Is travel insurance compulsory for Singapore residents?

No Singapore law requires it. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs advises every traveller to buy a policy that covers the planned activities, states that it does not provide loans or cash for medical, legal, accommodation or travel expenses abroad, and notes that some countries impose insurance as an entry requirement. Insurers may decline cover for destinations MFA advises against visiting.

When does cover start?

Trip cancellation, postponement and travel agency insolvency cover start on the policy issue date, or the booking date on an annual plan. Personal accident starts when the traveller leaves home for the airport or 3 hours before departure, whichever is later, on one major wording. Medical, delay and baggage cover start on departure from Singapore and end on return. Some insurers sell per-trip cover up to 1 day after departure.

Are pre-existing conditions covered?

Not on standard plans. Most exclude any condition for which the traveller had treatment, medication or symptoms in the 12 months before the trip. Dedicated pre-existing plans cover stable conditions, with limits such as S$150,000 overseas medical on one insurer and a 25% co-payment on travel inconvenience claims on another, and trip caps of 30 to 45 days. Conditions with a terminal prognosis under 12 months stay excluded.

Single trip or annual plan?

An annual plan covers unlimited trips for 12 months, each capped at 90 consecutive days on most wordings, and costs less than three or more single-trip policies. Single-trip plans allow longer trips, to 180 days, and are the only plans that carry cancel-for-any-reason riders, which must be bought within 7 to 30 days of the first deposit depending on the insurer. Multi-country trips returning to Singapore between legs need separate single-trip policies.

What is paid for a flight delay?

A fixed amount per block of consecutive hours, not the cost incurred. Current Singapore wordings pay S$100 per 6 hours or S$50 per 3 hours, to plan caps of S$300 to S$2,000. The delay must be caused by weather, strikes, mechanical breakdown or another listed reason and confirmed in writing by the carrier. Baggage delay pays a similar fixed sum per 6 hours after arrival overseas.

Does it cover skiing, diving or trekking?

Recreational activities open to the public are covered on most Singapore plans, including bungee jumping, scuba diving with a licensed operator, horse riding, winter sports and white-water rafting, provided safety rules are followed. Professional, competitive or extreme sports are excluded, as is diving below the depth stated in the wording. Sports equipment hire and loss need a separate rider on most plans.
