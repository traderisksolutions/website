The cover

Motor insurance in Singapore

Motor insurance covers the owner's legal liability to other road users and, on wider plans, the car itself. The Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act makes it an offence to use a vehicle in Singapore without a policy covering death or bodily injury to third parties; the penalty is a fine of up to S$1,000, up to 3 months in prison, or both, plus 12 months' disqualification. LTA will not renew road tax unless a policy covers the whole renewal period, so every owner buys it each year.

Motor insurance is not a warranty and does not pay for wear, mechanical failure or a driver who was drunk or unlicensed. Three tiers are sold: third-party only, third-party fire and theft, and comprehensive, which adds damage to the car itself. Every policy carries an excess, a no-claim discount from 10% after one claim-free year to 50% after five, and reporting rules under the GIA Motor Claims Framework. Who buys it

  • Private car owners renewing with road tax each year.
  • Drivers under 27 or with under 1 year of experience, who face a higher unnamed-driver excess.
  • Owners of older cars deciding between comprehensive and third-party fire and theft.

Price and terms

What moves the premium

No-claim discount
Each claim-free year adds 10% off the premium up to 50% after 5 years; an at-fault claim drops 50% to 20%, 40% to 10% and 30% or below to 0%.
Driver age and experience
Drivers under 27 or with under 1 year on the licence pay more and carry a higher excess, S$2,500 on one major wording where unnamed.
Vehicle make, age and value
Repair cost and parts availability set the own-damage rate; performance and electric models cost more to repair.
Cover tier
Third-party only is cheapest; fire and theft and then comprehensive add cover and premium.
Excess
Raising the standard S$600 excess lowers the premium; buying it down to zero raises it.
Workshop choice
Any-workshop plans cost more than authorised-workshop plans; some tiers tie repairs to named workshops.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Reporting late
The Motor Claims Framework requires a report to the insurer and the car at an approved reporting centre within 24 hours or by the next working day, even for a private settlement. Miss it and the insurer can repudiate the claim, cut the NCD or refuse renewal.
Leaving a young driver unnamed
An unnamed driver under 27 or with under 1 year of experience triggers an excess of S$2,500 on many policies. Name every regular driver and check the excess for each.
Using the wrong workshop
Repairs at a workshop the insurer has not authorised can void the own-damage claim, even when the other party is at fault. Call the insurer hotline for towing and send the car where the policy says.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What is the minimum motor insurance required by law in Singapore?

A policy from an insurer licensed in Singapore covering liability for death or bodily injury to third parties arising from use of the vehicle. The Act does not require cover for third-party property damage, but every policy sold in Singapore includes it, up to S$5,000,000 on one major wording. Driving without the policy carries a fine of up to S$1,000, up to 3 months in prison, or both, and 12 months' disqualification unless the court finds special reasons.

How does the no-claim discount work?

Each claim-free year adds 10% to the discount, to a cap of 50% after 5 years. One at-fault claim drops 50% to 20%, 40% to 10%, and 30% or lower to 0%; two claims in a year reset it to 0%. The discount belongs to the driver, not the car, and transfers between insurers on an NCD letter. An NCD protector add-on preserves it for the first claim, offered from 30% on most plans.

What must I do after an accident?

Exchange particulars, photograph the scene and the damage before moving the cars, and call the insurer hotline for towing. Report to the insurer and bring the car to an approved reporting centre within 24 hours or by the next working day, even if there is no damage or the parties settle privately. Make a police report within 24 hours if anyone is injured, or a pedestrian, cyclist, foreign vehicle or government property is involved, or it is a hit-and-run.

What is the difference between the three cover tiers?

Third-party only covers liability for injury, death and property damage caused to others. Third-party fire and theft adds loss of the car to fire or theft. Comprehensive adds accidental damage to the car itself, windscreen, towing and usually a personal accident benefit for the driver. The choice turns on the value of the car against the extra premium; insurers do not offer comprehensive terms on every older car.

Can I drive into Malaysia on my Singapore policy?

Most Singapore policies extend third-party cover to West Malaysia, and insurers sell a rider that adds towing, higher medical limits and own-damage benefits there and up to 80 km into Thailand on one wording. Check the policy map before each trip. An accident involving a Malaysian-registered vehicle requires a police report, and towing back from Malaysia is arranged under the insurer's own terms.

Does my NCD transfer if I switch insurer or buy a new car?

Yes. The discount belongs to the policyholder. The new insurer takes it from the NCD letter or the expiring policy, and it moves with the owner to a replacement car. Some insurers allow a transfer between spouses or from an overseas insurer with proof. Late reporting of an accident drops the NCD one further level on one major wording, so report every incident on time.
