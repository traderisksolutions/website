The cover

Maid insurance in Singapore

Maid insurance is the package an employer of a migrant domestic worker must hold under Ministry of Manpower rules: medical insurance of at least S$60,000 a year for inpatient care and day surgery, personal accident insurance with a sum assured of at least S$60,000 payable to the helper or her beneficiaries, and a S$5,000 security bond for every helper who is not Malaysian. All three must be in force before the helper arrives in Singapore, or she is not allowed to enter.

It is not health insurance for the employer's family and does not cover the helper's outpatient GP visits unless the plan adds them. Policies are sold for 14 or 26 months to match 12 and 24 month work permits. Under the enhanced medical requirement, the insurer pays 75% of hospital costs above S$15,000 and the employer 25%, unless a co-payment waiver is added. TRS compares the hospital limit, the waiver, wage and repatriation benefits and the bond protector, then has the insurer issue the bond to MOM.

Who buys it

  • First-time employers applying for a work permit for a helper.
  • Employers renewing a 2-year work permit, where the policy must restart with it.
  • Transfer employers taking over a helper already in Singapore.
  • Employers of helpers above 50, where age-banded premiums apply from 1 July 2025.
  • Employers who want hospital costs above the MOM minimum covered.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Helper's passport, nationality and date of birth
  • Work permit application or renewal reference
  • Permit term: 12 or 24 months
  • Employer's NRIC and address
  • Arrival date, or current permit expiry for renewals
  • Existing policy to be replaced on transfer

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Helper's age
Since 1 July 2025 MOM allows two premium bands, age 50 and below and above 50; the older band costs more.
Policy term
A 26-month policy for a 2-year permit costs less per month than two 14-month policies.
Hospital limit above the minimum
Raising the S$60,000 annual limit or buying a co-payment waiver adds premium.
Optional benefits
Wage reimbursement, repatriation, replacement helper costs and outpatient cover each add to the base rate.
Security bond protector
A rider that limits the employer's liability on a bond forfeiture, added within 30 days of the policy start on most plans, carries its own premium.
Pre-existing conditions
Some insurers cover a helper who has worked in Singapore for more than 12 consecutive months; the wider wording costs more.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Buying after the helper lands
MOM requires the medical, accident and bond cover to take effect on arrival, and the insurer needs up to 3 working days to transmit the bond to MOM. Buy at least a week before the flight and keep the MOM confirmation.
Stopping at the minimum
A S$60,000 limit with 25% co-payment above S$15,000 leaves the employer paying thousands on a long admission, and the cost cannot be deducted from salary. Add the co-payment waiver or a higher hospital limit.
Forgetting the bond on renewal
The security bond and both policies must run for the whole permit. Align the 26-month policy with the renewed permit date so no gap opens before MOM discharges the old bond.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What insurance does MOM require for a domestic helper?

Three items. Medical insurance with an annual claim limit of at least S$60,000 covering inpatient care and day surgery, with the insurer paying 75% and the employer 25% of costs above S$15,000. Personal accident insurance with a sum assured of at least S$60,000, payable to the helper or her beneficiaries. A S$5,000 security bond for each helper who is not Malaysian. All must be in force before she arrives.

What changed in the MOM medical insurance rules in 2023 and 2025?

From 1 July 2023 the minimum annual limit rose to S$60,000 with the 75/25 co-payment above S$15,000. From 1 July 2025 insurers must use standardised exclusion clauses, may price in two age bands (50 and below, above 50) and must pay hospitals directly once a claim is admissible. The rules apply to policies, renewals and extensions starting on or after each date.

What is the security bond and when do I get it back?

A S$5,000 guarantee to MOM for each non-Malaysian helper, usually issued by the insurer with the maid policy rather than paid in cash. MOM may forfeit it if the employer or helper breaches work permit conditions, salary is paid late, the helper is not sent home when the permit ends, or she goes missing. It is discharged about one week after she leaves Singapore, provided the permit was cancelled and no condition was breached.

Why is the policy 14 or 26 months when the permit is 12 or 24?

Insurers add 2 months so cover runs through the period after the permit expires, when the helper may still be in Singapore awaiting repatriation or transfer, and so the bond stays in force until MOM discharges it. The extra months are priced into the plan at no separate premium. The 26-month policy is the usual match for a fresh or renewed 2-year permit.

Who pays the helper's medical bills above the insurance?

The employer. MOM holds employers responsible for the helper's medical needs and does not allow medical expenses to be deducted from her salary. Above the S$60,000 annual limit, and for the 25% co-payment on costs above S$15,000, the employer pays unless the policy has a co-payment waiver or a higher limit. Outpatient GP visits are the employer's cost unless the plan includes an outpatient benefit.

Does the cover apply on her rest day or outside the home?

Personal accident cover under MOM rules responds to sudden accidents causing death or permanent disability, and the policy may not carry exclusions beyond those in the Employment of Foreign Manpower Regulations. Hospital cover applies to admissions in Singapore. Every helper has at least one rest day a month that cannot be compensated away, since 1 January 2023; check the wording for injuries away from the home on that day before buying.
