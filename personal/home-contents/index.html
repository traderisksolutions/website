The cover

Home contents insurance in Singapore

Home contents insurance pays to repair or replace what is inside the home: furniture, appliances, electronics, clothing and the renovations the owner paid for. The perils are fire, lightning, explosion, burst pipes, flood, theft with forced entry and impact; all-risks plans add accidental damage. Owners buy it because the HDB Fire Insurance Scheme and bank mortgagee policies cover the structure only. Tenants buy it for their own belongings and their liability to the landlord. Landlords buy it for the fittings they leave in a leased unit.

It is not fire insurance on the structure and does not replace the HDB or bank policy. Cover is bought for one year on a sum insured for contents, a separate sum for renovations and a liability limit, with an excess per claim. Most Singapore plans pay new-for-old; some settle on a first-loss basis so a low sum insured is not penalised. TRS matches the renovation sum to the contractor's invoice, checks the sub-limits on valuables and electronics, and places the policy with the insurer whose wording fits the home.

Who buys it

  • HDB and condominium owners whose fire policy stops at the HDB-provided structure.
  • Owners who have completed a renovation and need the works insured at cost.
  • Tenants insuring their own belongings and their liability to the landlord.
  • Landlords insuring fittings and loss of rent on a leased unit.
  • Households holding jewellery, watches, cameras or bicycles above the standard sub-limits.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Address, property type and whether owner, tenant or landlord
  • Renovation cost with the contractor invoice or quotation
  • Estimated replacement value of contents
  • List of items above the per-article sub-limit
  • Claims or losses in the last 3 years
  • Existing HDB fire or mortgagee policy details

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Sum insured for contents
Plans run from S$35,000 to S$100,000 of contents on standard tiers; a higher figure raises the premium in step.
Renovation sum
Built-in carpentry, flooring and kitchens are insured at invoice cost; a S$100,000 renovation costs more to insure than a S$30,000 one.
Insured perils or all-risks
An all-risks plan adds accidental damage and costs more than a named-perils plan.
Valuables
Jewellery and watches are capped per item, often 5% of the contents sum or a fixed figure, and specifying higher amounts adds premium.
Liability limit
Family liability limits range from S$500,000 to S$5,000,000 between insurers and affect the price.
Policy term
Some insurers discount 3-year terms against annual renewal.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Under-insuring the contents
On an average-clause policy, insuring S$40,000 of contents worth S$50,000 cuts every claim to 80%. Total the replacement cost room by room, or choose a first-loss plan that waives the condition.
Leaving renovations out
Fire insurance pays for the HDB-provided floor, not the marble laid over it. Insure renovations at invoice cost under their own section and update the sum after each new job.
Missing the sub-limits
A S$6,000 watch claimed on a plan with a S$2,500 per-article cap pays S$2,500. Declare valuables above the cap and have them listed on the schedule.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

I have HDB fire insurance. Do I still need home contents insurance?

Yes, if the renovations, furniture and belongings matter. The HDB policy reinstates only the structure, fixtures and fittings HDB provided. CPF Board's guide to insuring an HDB flat recommends a separate contents policy for renovations, furniture and appliances, and notes that private policies can also meet debris removal and alternative living costs. The HDB policy pays none of these.

What is the difference between insured perils and all-risks cover?

An insured-perils plan pays only for the events listed: fire, lightning, explosion, burst pipes, flood, theft by forcible entry and impact. An all-risks plan pays for any sudden accidental loss not excluded, such as a dropped television. All-risks costs more and still excludes wear, gradual leaks, pests in the first 30 days on one wording, and homes left unoccupied for more than 60 consecutive days.

How much should I insure my contents for?

The cost to replace everything new today, room by room. Insurers settle new-for-old, so the figure is replacement cost, not resale value. Where the policy carries an average clause, under-insuring reduces every claim in the same proportion. Where it is first-loss, the sum is the largest single loss the household expects to claim and the average clause does not apply.

Does home contents insurance cover my family's liability or my domestic helper?

Most plans include family personal liability, from S$500,000 to S$5,000,000 depending on the insurer, for injury or property damage caused to others by the family and, on many wordings, by a domestic helper working in the home. Tenant plans add liability to the landlord for damage to the unit, up to S$500,000 on one wording. The helper's own medical and accident cover is a separate MOM-mandated maid policy.

Are jewellery, laptops and bicycles covered?

Yes, within sub-limits. One major insurer caps laptops and tablets at S$1,500 per item and S$5,000 in total, mobile phones at S$500 per item, bicycles at S$1,000, and valuables at 5% of the contents sum per item up to one-third of the sum in total. Another caps jewellery and watches at S$1,750 to S$5,000 per article by plan tier. Items above these must be declared and listed.

Can a tenant buy home contents insurance?

Yes. A tenant insures their own furniture, electronics and belongings, any renovation they paid for, and their liability to the landlord for damage to the unit. The landlord's structure and fittings stay on the landlord's policy. The cover moves with the tenant to a new address once the insurer is told, and the lease usually names the tenant as responsible for the landlord's property in their care.
