The cover

Personal accident insurance in Singapore

Personal accident insurance pays a fixed lump sum if an accident causes death or permanent disablement, and reimburses medical bills from an accident: clinic visits, X-rays, physiotherapy and, on many plans, Traditional Chinese Medicine. It pays on the event, not on proof of lost income. Singapore residents buy it because MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plans are built around hospital admissions, so an outpatient fracture or a ligament repair in a day clinic falls outside them. Self-employed people, delivery riders and sports players buy it most.

It is not life insurance and not health insurance: illness is excluded unless the plan adds named infectious diseases such as dengue, and pre-existing conditions are excluded. Cover is bought for one year on a sum insured, a medical reimbursement limit per accident and optional weekly or daily cash benefits. Premiums follow occupation class rather than age on most plans. TRS checks the disablement scale, the activity exclusions and the claim deadline, and places the cover with the insurer whose wording fits the buyer's work and sport.

Who buys it

  • Self-employed people and gig workers with no employer medical or work injury cover.
  • Employees whose company plan stops at hospitalisation and excludes outpatient treatment.
  • Families insuring children against playground, cycling and sports injuries.
  • Motorcyclists, climbers and divers whose activities need an explicit extension.
  • Retirees who have lost employer cover and want accident cover without medical underwriting.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Occupation and a one-line description of duties
  • Age and residency status of each person insured
  • Sum insured and medical limit wanted
  • Sports or activities practised regularly
  • Existing accident, health or employer cover
  • Accident claims in the last 3 years

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Occupation class
Office work is class 1; manual, outdoor or height work is rated higher and some trades are declined.
Sum insured
The lump sum for death or permanent disablement, from S$100,000 to S$1,000,000 on current plans, sets the base rate.
Medical reimbursement limit
A S$3,000 per-accident limit costs more than S$1,000; adding TCM and physiotherapy adds further.
Weekly or daily benefits
Temporary disablement income, such as S$200 a week for up to 104 weeks, and hospital cash are priced add-ons.
Activities
Motorcycling, aerial sports and diving below 40 metres are excluded or loaded.
Family cover
Spouse discounts of 10% and free child cover up to a share of the adult sum reduce the per-person cost.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Assuming illness is covered
A personal accident plan pays for accidents; dengue, HFMD and COVID-19 are covered only where named, and often after a waiting period of 14 days or more from policy start. Read the infectious disease schedule before relying on it.
Wrong occupation class
A rider declared as an office worker is rated at the wrong class, and the insurer can reject or reduce the claim. State the real job and tell the insurer when it changes.
Claiming late
Policies set a notification window, commonly 30 days from the accident, and want original receipts and the medical report. Report the accident at once and keep every bill.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What does personal accident insurance pay for?

A lump sum on accidental death, a percentage of the sum insured on permanent disablement according to a scale in the policy, and reimbursement of medical costs from an accident up to a per-accident limit. Many plans add weekly cash during temporary disablement, daily hospital cash and named infectious diseases. Payment does not depend on a hospital stay, which is the gap it fills next to MediShield Life.

How is this different from my Integrated Shield Plan?

An Integrated Shield Plan reimburses hospital and day-surgery bills, after a deductible and co-insurance, and does not pay for most outpatient clinic, physiotherapy or TCM treatment. A personal accident plan pays a fixed sum on death or disablement regardless of bills, and reimburses outpatient accident treatment. The two overlap only on an accident that leads to admission, where both can respond.

Does it cover dengue or COVID-19?

Only where the plan names the disease. Several Singapore insurers list dengue and 20 or more other infectious diseases as a benefit with a fixed payout or medical reimbursement, usually after a waiting period from policy start; one insurer applies 14 days for COVID-19 cover on selected plans. Illness outside the named list is excluded on every personal accident plan.

Will it pay if I am injured riding a motorcycle or playing sport?

Recreational sport open to the public is normally covered. Common exclusions are aerial activities, underwater activities below 40 metres, racing, speed or time trials and any sport for financial reward. Motorcycling is excluded or loaded on some plans and covered on others. Declare the activity before buying and get the extension in writing; an undeclared risk is the usual reason a claim is reduced.

What is an occupation class and why does it change my premium?

Insurers group jobs by accident frequency. Class 1 is indoor office work; higher classes cover sales staff on the road, technicians, drivers, construction and offshore trades. The premium for the same sum insured rises with the class, and a few trades are declined. The class on the schedule must match the actual job, or the insurer can reduce the claim in proportion to the premium underpaid.

How do I claim, and what if the insurer refuses?

Notify the insurer within the policy deadline, commonly 30 days, and submit the claim form, medical report and original receipts; one insurer acknowledges within 3 working days and pays approved claims by PayNow within 3 business days. If the claim is rejected, FIDReC adjudicates disputes up to S$150,000 per claim at no charge to the consumer, provided the complaint is filed within 6 months of the insurer's final reply.

Sources: FIDReC, jurisdiction and claim limits, SDIC, Policy Owners' Protection Scheme scope, Singlife, Personal Accident policy page
