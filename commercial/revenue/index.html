The cover

Business interruption insurance in Singapore

Business interruption insurance replaces the gross profit a business loses while it cannot trade after physical damage to its premises. In Singapore it is sold as a consequential loss policy alongside a fire policy, or as Section II of an industrial all risks policy. Landlords rarely ask for it; banks lending against a factory sometimes do. Most buyers add it after a neighbour's fire, a flood or a long reinstatement shows that the property claim pays for the building and nothing else.

It is not cover for a pandemic, a cyber outage, a supplier's price rise or a closure without damage: the trigger is damage insured under the property policy, with that insurer having paid or admitted the claim. The buyer sets a sum insured on gross profit, picks an indemnity period in months and accepts a time excess. The broker checks the gross profit calculation against the accounts, tests the indemnity period against a realistic rebuild and runs the claim with the loss adjuster.

Who buys it

  • Manufacturers whose single plant in Singapore produces all their output.
  • F&B and retail groups with high rent and payroll that continue during a closure.
  • Logistics operators running one warehouse under a long customer contract.
  • Clinics, laboratories and data-heavy firms whose fit-out takes months to rebuild.
  • Companies with a bank covenant that requires loss of profits cover.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Last two years of audited accounts with turnover and gross profit
  • List of standing charges to be insured: rent, salaries, loan interest
  • Indemnity period requested, with the rebuild estimate behind it
  • Underlying property policy: insurer, sum insured, perils
  • Key suppliers and customers, with share of turnover
  • Any alternative premises or contract production available

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Gross profit sum insured
Premium is a rate on the gross profit insured, so turnover and the margin after variable costs set the base.
Indemnity period
A 24-month period costs more than 12 months because the sum insured and the exposure both double.
Underlying property rate
The consequential loss rate follows the fire or IAR rate, so construction, occupancy and sprinklers move it.
Dependency on one site
A business that can shift production to a second site or a contract manufacturer rates lower.
Rebuild time
A long reinstatement, special plant with long lead times or a cleanroom pushes the period and the price up.
Extensions
Suppliers, customers, utilities, denial of access and wages on a dual basis each add premium.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Gross profit taken from the accounts
Accounting gross profit deducts labour and overheads that continue during a closure, so the insured figure is too low and average cuts the claim. Use the policy definition: turnover plus closing stock, less opening stock and the working expenses listed as uninsured.
Indemnity period that ends before trading recovers
Twelve months covers the rebuild but not the SCDF certification, re-fit, re-hiring and winning customers back. Set the period from a written timeline for the worst site, then add a margin; manufacturers with imported plant usually need 18 or 24 months.
Sum insured matched to 12 months when the period is longer
With an 18- or 24-month indemnity period the policy compares the sum insured with gross profit multiplied for that period, and applies average on the shortfall. Insure the gross profit for the whole indemnity period, not for one year.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

How is a business interruption claim calculated?

The insurer applies the rate of gross profit from the last financial year to the shortfall in turnover during the indemnity period, measured against the same months of the prior year and adjusted for the trend of the business. It adds increased cost of working spent to limit the shortfall, capped at the gross profit that spending saved, and deducts any charges that stopped during the closure.

What is the indemnity period?

The indemnity period starts on the date of the damage and runs until the results of the business stop being affected, up to the maximum stated in the schedule. It is not the policy period. The policy lasts 12 months; the indemnity period is chosen by the buyer and can be 12, 18, 24 or 36 months. It must cover reinstatement plus the time to recover trade.

What is increased cost of working?

Extra spending made for the sole purpose of avoiding or reducing a fall in turnover: temporary premises, overtime, outsourced production, express freight. The standard wording pays it only up to the gross profit it preserved, the economic limit. Additional increased cost of working, bought as an extension, removes that limit up to a stated sum.

Does the policy cover a closure ordered by the authorities?

Not unless there is insured damage. Singapore consequential loss wordings require physical damage at the premises insured under the property policy, with that insurer having paid or admitted liability. A closure for an outbreak, a government order or a neighbour's fire without damage to the insured premises is outside cover unless a denial of access or public authority extension is bought.

Can staff wages be insured?

Yes. Wages can sit inside gross profit as an insured standing charge, or be insured separately on a dual basis: 100% of wages for an initial number of weeks, then a lower percentage for the rest of the indemnity period, with an option to carry unused weeks forward. The dual basis suits manufacturers who must keep skilled staff while a plant is rebuilt.

What records does the insurer need at claim time?

Books of account, management accounts, invoices, bank statements and the auditor's certification of turnover and gross profit; the wording lets the insured recover reasonable auditor fees as a separate item. The written claim is due within 30 days after the indemnity period ends, and interim payments can be made monthly against accounts of the loss.
