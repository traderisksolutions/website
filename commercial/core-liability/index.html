The cover

Public and products liability insurance in Singapore

Public and products liability insurance pays the compensation and defence costs a business owes when a third party is injured or their property is damaged because of its operations or products. In Singapore the purchase is usually forced by a contract. Landlords ask for it in the lease before handing over keys. Main contractors and government tenders ask for it before a sub-contractor starts on site. Retailers, F&B operators, event companies, contractors, logistics firms and manufacturers buy it for this reason.

It is not cover for the insured's own staff. Work injuries are compensated under the Work Injury Compensation Act, which needs its own policy. Public liability is written on an occurrence basis: the policy in force when the injury or damage happens responds, however late the claim arrives. The buyer chooses a limit per occurrence and in aggregate, a deductible, a territory and a jurisdiction. The broker reads the lease or tender clause, matches the limit and the principal's name to it, and places the policy with the insurer whose wording fits the trade.

Who buys it

  • Tenants of retail, office or industrial units whose lease requires public liability cover naming the landlord.
  • Contractors and sub-contractors who must show a policy before a tender award or site access.
  • Manufacturers, importers and distributors whose products reach consumers in Singapore or overseas.
  • F&B, event and hospitality operators with public footfall on their premises.
  • Logistics, cleaning, facility and security firms working on client sites.
  • Clinics, schools and enrichment centres with visitors and children on the premises.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Description of activities, premises and any work at height or hot works
  • Last three years' turnover and the current year's estimate
  • Lease or contract clause stating the required limit and principal
  • Claims and incident history for the last five years
  • Product list, markets sold into and any recalls
  • Sub-contractor use and their own insurance certificates

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Trade
A hot-works contractor or a crowd event carries more injury risk than an office tenant and pays a higher rate on the same limit.
Limit of indemnity
The per-occurrence and aggregate limits drive the premium; insurers price the figure in the lease or tender clause.
Turnover and payroll
Insurers rate on annual turnover or wages, so a new contract or site changes the premium at renewal.
Products and markets
Goods sold into the USA or Canada are priced separately or excluded; territory and jurisdiction follow the sales map.
Claims history
Five years of claims experience sets the rate and the deductible; a clean record earns a lower excess.
Deductible
A higher excess per claim lowers the premium and keeps small property-damage claims off the record.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Limit set below the contract
The lease or tender states a limit per occurrence and the policy carries a lower one, so the certificate is rejected or the shortfall is uninsured. Read the clause before placement and match the limit, the principal name and the cross-liability wording.
Staff injuries claimed under the wrong policy
A worker is hurt on site and the claim goes to the public liability insurer, which declines it because employees are excluded. Work injuries belong under a Work Injury Compensation policy, which is compulsory for every manual worker and every non-manual employee earning S$2,600 a month or less.
Territory stops at the border
A product reaches a customer overseas or a crew works in Johor and the policy covers Singapore only. Set territory and jurisdiction to match where the goods and the people go, and declare any North American sales.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Is public liability insurance compulsory in Singapore?

No law makes it compulsory. Work Injury Compensation insurance is compulsory for employees; public liability is not. Most purchases are driven by a contract: a lease, a tender, a mall operator's fit-out rules or a client's vendor terms. If a lease or tender states a minimum limit, the business must hold it before the keys or the site are handed over.

What is the difference between public liability and products liability?

Public liability covers injury or damage caused by the business's operations and premises. Products liability covers injury or damage caused by goods after they have left the business's control, including goods it imports or distributes under its own name. Both are usually written in one policy with one combined limit. A manufacturer or importer needs the products section; a pure service firm may not.

Does it cover injuries to my own employees?

No. Employees are excluded. Their injuries fall under the Work Injury Compensation Act, which for accidents from 1 November 2025 caps compensation at S$269,000 for death, S$346,000 for permanent incapacity and S$53,000 for medical expenses. Employers must insure all manual workers and non-manual employees earning S$2,600 a month or less under a separate policy, or face a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both.

What limit do landlords and tenders ask for?

The figure is set in the lease or the tender conditions, not by law. It is usually stated per occurrence, with the landlord or principal named as an additional insured and a cross-liability clause. Read the clause before buying. If the policy limit is lower than the clause, the certificate is rejected at handover or award and the gap is uninsured.

Is public liability written on a claims-made or occurrence basis?

Occurrence. The policy in force on the date the injury or damage happened pays, even if the claim is lodged years later. That differs from professional indemnity, which is claims-made and responds only if a policy is live when the claim arrives. Keep expired policy schedules on file, because a claim for an old incident goes back to that year's insurer.

Does the policy pay legal costs?

Yes. Defence costs incurred with the insurer's consent are covered, including a Singapore court action. Whether those costs sit inside the limit or on top of it depends on the wording. With costs inside the limit, a long defence leaves less for the award itself. Ask for costs in addition to the limit where the contract limit is tight.
