The cover

Stock throughput insurance in Singapore

Stock throughput insurance is a marine cargo policy extended to cover inventory while it is stored, not only while it moves. One policy follows raw materials, work in progress and finished goods from supplier to warehouse to customer, at owned sites and third-party logistics (3PL) warehouses worldwide. Singapore distributors, electronics and pharmaceutical manufacturers and commodity traders buy it when stock passes through several hands and separate cargo and property policies leave a gap at each handover.

It is not a replacement for property insurance on buildings, plant or business interruption, and damage caused by the manufacturing process itself is excluded. The policy is bought on all-risks cargo conditions with a limit per location and per conveyance, an annual aggregate for flood, windstorm and earthquake, and a single rate applied to sales or stock values declared through the year. The broker maps every storage point, agrees valuation and deductibles by stage, and removes stock from the property policy so that limit can fall.

Who buys it

  • Importers and distributors holding stock in 3PL warehouses in Singapore, Malaysia and China.
  • Electronics, semiconductor and medical device makers with high-value components in transit and in store.
  • Commodity traders with cargo at port, in bonded warehouses and on vessels at the same time.
  • Retail groups moving stock from distribution centres to many outlets.
  • Companies whose property insurer imposes percentage deductibles on stock for flood or storm.
  • Exporters with consignment stock held at customer sites overseas.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Schedule of every storage location with peak and average stock values
  • Annual sales and purchases split by transit and storage
  • Goods description, packing and temperature requirements
  • Warehouse construction, fire protection and security at each site
  • 3PL contracts showing who bears risk and any liability caps
  • Current cargo and property policy wordings and the loss record

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Peak stock value by location
The highest value at any one site sets the per-location limit and the catastrophe aggregate; concentration in one warehouse raises the rate.
Annual sales or throughput
Premium is a single rate on declared sales or stock values, adjusted at year end; higher turnover on the same peak stock lowers the effective rate.
Goods and process
Theft-attractive, perishable or temperature-controlled goods rate higher; processing stages are covered but process damage is excluded.
Warehouse quality
Sprinklers, alarms, flood height and 3PL contract terms move the rate and decide whether a location is accepted at full limit.
Deductible structure
Flat-dollar deductibles by stage replace percentage property deductibles; a higher deductible on static stock cuts premium more than one on transit.
Territories and catastrophe exposure
Locations in flood, typhoon or earthquake zones carry annual aggregate sublimits and a loading on the rate.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Leaving stock on the property policy
Insuring the same stock under both policies creates a contribution dispute at claim time and pays premium twice. Remove stock from the property sum insured once the stock throughput policy incepts, and tell both insurers in writing.
Missing a location
A warehouse not on the schedule has no limit, and an unscheduled 3PL site may be uninsured. Review the location schedule each quarter and declare new sites before stock moves in.
Assuming the 3PL is liable
Standard trading conditions cap a logistics provider's liability at a few dollars per kilogram, far below stock value. Insure the stock yourself and let the insurer recover against the 3PL under the policy's subrogation clause.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

How does stock throughput differ from marine cargo plus property insurance?

A cargo policy ends when goods are unloaded at the final warehouse, or 60 days after discharge at the final port. A property policy covers stock only at named premises, often with percentage deductibles and exclusions for goods in a third party's care. Stock throughput joins the two: ocean cargo, inland transit and storage under one all-risks wording, one valuation basis and one deductible structure, so no handover is uninsured.

Is stock in a 3PL warehouse covered?

Yes, when the site is on the location schedule and within its limit. Property insurance often excludes delay, damage or theft while goods are in a third party's possession; a stock throughput policy is written to include it. The insurer will ask for the 3PL contract, the warehouse's fire and security standards and the value held there at peak.

How is stock valued at claim time?

The valuation clause sets it. Raw materials are usually valued at replacement cost plus freight and duty; finished goods can be valued at selling price less costs not incurred, which protects the margin a property policy would not pay. The basis is agreed at inception for each category of stock and should match how stock is carried in the accounts.

What limits and deductibles apply?

The policy carries a limit per location, a limit per conveyance and an annual aggregate for catastrophe perils such as flood, windstorm and earthquake. Deductibles are flat dollar amounts that can differ by stage: lower on transit, higher on static stock in a sprinklered warehouse. This replaces the percentage-of-sum-insured deductible that property policies apply to stock.

Does it cover goods while being processed?

Goods are covered while in process at the insured's premises, but damage caused by the manufacturing process itself, such as a failed batch or a machine fault, is excluded. Fire, flood, theft or impact during processing is covered. Manufacturers with high-value work in progress should check the process exclusion wording and the limit at the production site.

Which Singapore companies qualify?

Insurers write stock throughput for companies with regular transit flows and meaningful static stock: importers, distributors, manufacturers and traders with inventory at more than one site. There is no fixed minimum, but the policy is priced on a single rate across sales, so a business with little transit and all stock in one owned warehouse is usually better served by a property policy.
