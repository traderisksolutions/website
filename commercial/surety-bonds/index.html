The cover

Surety bonds in Singapore

A surety bond is a guarantee issued by an insurer to a project owner, government agency or customs authority that a business will meet an obligation: finish the works, honour a tender, use an advance correctly, or pay duty. Singapore contractors, subcontractors, M&E firms, facility managers and traders buy bonds because the contract or the law demands security: PSSCOC public-sector contracts call for a deposit or a bank or insurer guarantee, MOM requires a S$5,000 security bond for each non-Malaysian Work Permit holder, and Singapore Customs accepts an insurance bond for suspended duty.

A bond is not insurance for the contractor. If the beneficiary calls it, the insurer pays and then recovers from the contractor under a counter-indemnity signed at the outset. Bonds are issued one at a time or under an annual facility with an aggregate limit, at a rate on the bond amount for its term, with performance bonds usually at 5% to 10% of the contract sum. The broker arranges the facility, negotiates the bond wording with the beneficiary, and manages calls and releases.

Who buys it

  • Main contractors and subcontractors on PSSCOC and private construction contracts.
  • M&E, facade and fit-out specialists asked for performance and retention bonds.
  • Facility management and services firms on multi-year government contracts.
  • Employers of Work Permit holders needing MOM security bonds.
  • Importers, licensed warehouse operators and excise traders lodging security with Customs.
  • Companies whose bank guarantee lines are fully used or secured by cash.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Audited accounts for recent years and current management accounts
  • Contract, tender conditions and the bond wording demanded
  • Work-in-hand schedule with contract values and completion dates
  • Record of bonds issued, outstanding and ever called
  • Directors' and shareholders' details for the counter-indemnity
  • Bank facility letters showing existing guarantee lines

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Financial strength
Audited accounts, net worth and gearing set the facility limit and the rate; a weak balance sheet means collateral or no facility.
Bond type and wording
On-demand bonds, payable on written demand, rate higher than conditional bonds that need proof of breach and loss.
Bond amount and term
The rate applies to the bond value for its full term including any defects liability period; a 10% bond on a S$20 million contract runs on S$2 million.
Contract and beneficiary
Government and statutory board contracts on standard wordings place more readily than bespoke private wordings with wide call rights.
Track record
Completed projects of similar size, no past bond calls and no live disputes lower the rate.
Security offered
Personal guarantees from directors, cash collateral or a charge lower the rate and lift the facility limit.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Signing an on-demand wording unread
An on-demand bond pays on the beneficiary's written demand without proof of breach, and Singapore courts stop a call only for fraud or unconscionability. Negotiate a conditional wording where the contract allows, as the PSSCOC form does, and have the broker check every call right before issue.
Treating the bond as insurance
The insurer recovers every dollar paid on a call from the contractor under the counter-indemnity, often backed by directors' personal guarantees. Model a call as a cash event and keep the facility within what the balance sheet can repay.
Letting the bond expire or over-run
A bond that expires before the defects period ends breaches the contract; one left open after completion ties up the facility. Diarise expiry and release dates and ask the beneficiary for a discharge letter in writing.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What is the difference between a surety bond and a bank guarantee?

Both are accepted by Singapore government agencies, which ask for a bond from a bank or insurance company registered with MAS. A bank guarantee uses the company's credit line and is often secured by cash. A surety bond is issued by an insurer against a counter-indemnity, so it leaves bank lines and working capital free. Allianz Trade describes bonding as releasing bank working capital facilities and reducing security requirements.

How much is a performance bond in Singapore?

Typically 5% to 10% of the contract sum, as Tokio Marine Singapore states for its performance bond. PSSCOC 2020 clause 4.5 requires a security deposit of the amount in the contract appendix, or a bank or insurer guarantee in lieu; in SH Design & Build v Jurong Port [2026] SGHC 159 the figure was 10%. The bond usually runs to the end of the defects liability period.

What is the difference between an on-demand and a conditional bond?

An on-demand bond pays on the beneficiary's written demand; the insurer does not ask whether the contractor is in breach. A conditional bond pays only on proof of breach and loss. In AXA Insurance v Chiu Teng Construction [2021] SGCA 62 the Court of Appeal dealt with a conditional bond issued by an insurer and held a court judgment or admission was not needed before a valid call. The wording decides which type you hold.

Can an insurer issue the MOM security bond for foreign workers?

Yes. MOM requires a S$5,000 security bond for each non-Malaysian Work Permit holder, bought from any bank or insurance company before the worker arrives; the worker cannot be asked to pay for it. The bond is forfeited if the employer or worker breaches Work Permit conditions, and discharged about one week after the worker leaves Singapore if the permit is cancelled and no breach occurred.

Does Singapore Customs accept an insurance bond?

Yes. Customs accepts a Banker's Guarantee, a Finance Company Guarantee or an Insurance Bond, lodged electronically through eGuarantee@Gov since 1 October 2023 with a minimum validity of 15 months. The amount depends on the movement: 50% of potential duty for liquor and tobacco entering a licensed warehouse, for example. No security is needed when the amount would be S$2,000 or less.

What happens when a bond is called?

The beneficiary serves a written demand within the bond's terms. The insurer pays within the period the bond states, then claims the amount from the contractor under the counter-indemnity. The contractor can apply to court to restrain the call, but Singapore courts grant that only for fraud or unconscionability, or where the demand fails the bond's own conditions, as in SH Design & Build v Jurong Port, where an invalid superintending officer's decision defeated the call.

Sources: MOM: security bond for Work Permit holders, Singapore Customs: how to lodge security, BCA: Public Sector Standard Conditions of Contract, SH Design & Build v Jurong Port [2026] SGHC 159
