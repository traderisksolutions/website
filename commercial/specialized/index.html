The cover

Specialised asset insurance in Singapore

Contractors plant and machinery (CPM) insurance covers mobile cranes, crawler cranes, excavators, piling rigs, forklifts, boom lifts and other site plant against sudden accidental damage wherever they work in Singapore, whether owned or hired in. The purchase is forced by the contract: PSSCOC excludes construction equipment from the works insurance and public tenders require the contractor to insure its own plant, while plant hire agreements put the risk on the hirer from delivery to return.

It is not motor insurance, which the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act requires for anything used on a public road, and it is not breakdown cover: internal mechanical or electrical failure is excluded, though external damage that follows a breakdown is paid. Cover is annual, per item on a schedule, at new replacement value, with a deductible per occurrence. The broker adds transit, hired-in plant, third-party liability and road risks where the fleet needs them.

Who buys it

  • Crane and plant hire companies with fleets working on several sites at once.
  • Main contractors and piling, demolition and civil subcontractors that own heavy plant.
  • Logistics and port operators running forklifts, reach stackers and container handlers.
  • Shipyards, steel fabricators and precast yards with gantry cranes and mobile plant.
  • Contractors hiring in plant under agreements that make them responsible for loss or damage.
  • Owners of specialised units: concrete pumps, drilling rigs, mobile elevating work platforms.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Plant schedule: type, make, model, year, serial number, new replacement value
  • MOM registration and Authorised Examiner certificates for lifting equipment
  • Sites or areas where the plant will work, including marine or underground
  • Hire agreements for plant hired in, showing who bears the risk
  • Operator licences and lifting supervisor training records
  • Loss history for three years, including theft

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Replacement value of the fleet
Premium is a rate on the new replacement value of each listed item, so fleet size and age set the base.
Plant type
Cranes, piling rigs and plant working near water or underground rate higher than forklifts and compressors.
Where it works
Plant on marine, reclamation or tunnelling sites, or in transit between sites, needs endorsements that add premium.
Operator and maintenance records
Registered lifting equipment with current Authorised Examiner certificates and trained operators supports a lower rate.
Deductible
A deductible per occurrence, often a percentage of the item value with a minimum sum, lowers premium.
Extensions
Hired-in plant, third-party liability, overtime and express freight, and road risks are each rated on top.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Road-licensed plant with no motor policy
The CPM wording excludes vehicles licensed for general road use unless they work only on sites, and the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act requires a motor policy for road use. Lorry cranes and mobile cranes that drive between sites need motor cover for the road and CPM for the lift.
Transit between sites assumed covered
The standard wording excludes loss or damage while in transit unless endorsed, so an excavator that slides off a low-loader is not a claim. Add inland transit, or insure the move under the haulier's or a marine policy.
Plant hired in on the hirer's risk, insured by nobody
Hire agreements make the hirer responsible for the plant from delivery to return; the hirer assumes the owner insured it, and the owner's policy excludes items on hire. List hired-in plant on the CPM schedule or buy a hired-in plant extension with a limit that matches the largest unit.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Does the policy follow the plant from site to site?

Yes, within the geographical area stated in the schedule, usually Singapore, whether the item is working, at rest, or being dismantled, overhauled or re-erected. It responds only after the item has been commissioned. Transit between sites is excluded unless endorsed, and plant working underground or in tidal waters needs a specific endorsement.

Is mechanical breakdown covered?

No. Electrical or mechanical breakdown, failure, breakage, freezing of coolant and lack of lubrication are excluded. If the breakdown causes an accident with external damage, for example a boom that collapses after a hydraulic failure, the resulting external damage is paid. Breakdown of the plant itself needs a machinery breakdown policy.

How is a claim settled?

Repairable damage is paid at repair cost plus dismantling, re-erection and ordinary freight, with no deduction for depreciation on parts replaced. If repair cost reaches the actual value of the item before the loss, it is a total loss and the insurer pays that actual value, which is replacement value less depreciation. Notice must reach the insurer within 14 days of the loss, and the police must be informed for theft.

What does MOM require for cranes and lifting plant?

Lifting machines, lifts and hoists, lifting appliances and lifting gear must be registered with MOM through an Authorised Examiner, who tests the equipment, issues the examination report and registers it. Examples include mobile, crawler, tower and gantry cranes, lorry loaders, piling frames, mobile elevating work platforms and gondolas. Equipment is re-examined at the intervals set by the Workplace Safety and Health (General Provisions) Regulations or by the examiner. Insurers ask for the current certificate.

Does CPM cover third-party injury or damage caused by the plant?

Not under the standard wording, which pays for damage to the insured items only. Liability for a crane load that strikes a neighbouring building or a forklift that injures a visitor sits under the public liability policy or Section II of the project's CAR. Plant owners who hire out with operators buy a separate third-party liability section with its own limit.

Can I insure a single high-value unit rather than the whole fleet?

Yes. Each item is insured separately with its own sum insured and deductible, and average applies item by item, so a schedule can carry one tunnel boring machine, one concrete pump or one crawler crane. Insurers inspect single high-value units before cover and may set operating conditions. Premium is due within 60 days of inception under the premium payment warranty, or cover terminates.
