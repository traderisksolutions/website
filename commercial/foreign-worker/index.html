The cover

Foreign worker medical insurance in Singapore

Foreign worker medical insurance is the inpatient and day surgery policy MOM requires for every Work Permit and S Pass holder. It pays the hospital bill for illness or injury, work-related or not, up to a minimum of S$60,000 a year. The trigger is the pass itself: MOM will not issue or renew a Work Permit or S Pass until the employer has entered the insurer, policy number and dates in WP Online. Construction, marine, process, manufacturing, services and domestic employers all buy it.

It is not outpatient cover and it is not work injury compensation, which is a separate WICA policy. The policy is annual or matched to the pass, priced per worker in two age bands, 50 and below and above 50, and must follow the Stage 2 terms from 1 July 2025: insurer pays 75% of any claim above S$15,000, the employer 25%, and only MOM's allowable exclusions may apply. The broker places the headcount, checks every sub-limit meets S$60,000, adds a buy-back of the 25% where wanted, and keeps the declarations current.

Who buys it

  • Any employer of a Work Permit holder, before the permit is issued and at every renewal
  • Employers of S Pass holders, who face the same S$60,000 minimum
  • Construction, marine shipyard and process firms, who also enrol dormitory workers in a Primary Care Plan
  • Households employing a migrant domestic worker
  • Employers with workers on policies longer than two years, who must move to Stage 2 terms by 30 June 2027
  • Groups holding several policies that together must reach the S$60,000 and 75% tests

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Worker list with FIN, date of birth and pass type
  • Sector and occupation of each worker
  • Policy start date matched to the Work Permit or S Pass
  • Claims history for the group, two to three years
  • Existing policy schedules where several policies are combined
  • Whether a Primary Care Plan is already in place for dormitory or CMP workers

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Age band
Workers above 50 are priced in a separate, higher band under the Stage 2 rules.
Claim limit above the minimum
A S$100,000 or S$150,000 annual limit costs more than the S$60,000 floor but caps large bills.
Co-payment buy-back
A rider that takes the employer's 25% share above S$15,000 adds premium and removes the open exposure.
Headcount and sector
Large manual workforces in construction and marine carry higher claim frequency than services headcounts.
Claims experience
Group loss ratios from the last two or three years set the renewal rate and any loading.
Policy term
A 24-month policy matched to the Work Permit avoids a mid-term gap and a second administration cycle.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Treating the 25% co-payment as the worker's
The insurance cost cannot be passed to the worker, and a co-pay arrangement is allowed only if it is in the contract, with consent, capped at 10% of fixed monthly salary and no more than six months in every two years. Budget the employer share, or buy the buy-back rider.
Letting a sub-limit fall below S$60,000
MOM requires each sub-limit for inpatient, day surgery and per condition to meet the S$60,000 minimum, not only the overall annual cap. Read the schedule line by line before entering it in WP Online.
Assuming the worker's own plan or the Primary Care Plan is enough
An individual plan held by the worker does not discharge the employer's duty, and the Primary Care Plan covers only primary care. Buy the employer policy for every Work Permit and S Pass holder and keep the PCP alongside it where required.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What is the minimum cover in 2026?

At least S$60,000 a year for each Work Permit and S Pass holder, covering inpatient care and day surgery for conditions that may not be work-related, for the whole validity of the pass. Each sub-limit must also meet S$60,000. For claims above S$15,000 the insurer pays at least 75% and the employer 25%. Policies that started before 1 July 2023 could keep S$15,000 until their next renewal.

Can I pass the cost or the co-payment to the worker?

The premium cannot be recovered from the worker. A co-payment arrangement is permitted, except for migrant domestic workers, only where the amount is reasonable and at most 10% of the worker's fixed monthly salary, the duration is at most six months in every two years of employment, and the term is written into the employment contract or collective agreement with the worker's consent.

Can insurers still exclude pre-existing conditions?

Only within MOM's standardised list for policies starting on or after 1 July 2025. A pre-existing condition known to the employer or the worker may be excluded only in the first 12 months of employment with the same employer. Pregnancy, cosmetic procedures, non-accident dental, treatment outside Singapore and bills already paid under WICA remain allowable exclusions.

What is the Primary Care Plan and who needs it?

A capitated outpatient plan bought from an MOM-appointed anchor operator. It has been mandatory since 1 April 2022 for Work Permit and S Pass holders who live in dormitories or work in construction, marine shipyard or process. It covers consultations, telemedicine and an annual screening with a fixed S$5 co-payment per visit from 1 April 2027. It does not replace the medical insurance.

Does an S Pass or Employment Pass holder need this policy?

S Pass holders yes, on the same S$60,000 minimum and 75/25 co-payment as Work Permit holders, bought before the pass is issued or renewed. Employment Pass holders no; MOM does not require employer medical insurance for them, though many employers include them in the group medical plan.

Can I combine policies or buy back the 25%?

Yes to both. MOM accepts several policies together provided they give a yearly maximum of at least S$60,000 and insurers pay at least 75% above the first S$15,000. Employers may also buy riders that reimburse the 25% employer share up to the annual limit, so the exposure above S$15,000 is closed off.
