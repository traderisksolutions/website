The cover

Professional indemnity insurance in Singapore

Professional indemnity insurance pays the compensation and defence costs a firm owes when a client says its advice, design or service was negligent and caused financial loss. In Singapore it is bought by engineers, architects, consultants, accountants, IT and software firms, marketing agencies and fund managers. The trigger is usually a contract: a client's procurement terms or a tender demand a stated limit for the life of the engagement. Some professions must hold it to practise; advocates and solicitors are covered through the Law Society's compulsory scheme.

It is not cover for injury or property damage, which sits under public liability. It is written on a claims-made basis: the policy answers only to claims first made and notified while it is in force. A retroactive date sets how far back the covered work goes. The buyer selects a limit per claim and in aggregate, a deductible and an annual term. The broker checks the retroactive date and the client's clause before placing the policy, and arranges run-off cover when the firm closes or changes insurer.

Who buys it

  • Engineering, architectural and project management practices with design responsibility.
  • Management, HR, ESG and strategy consultants engaged under client procurement terms.
  • IT, software and SaaS firms whose contracts include errors and omissions clauses.
  • Accounting, tax and corporate secretarial firms acting for many clients at once.
  • Marketing, PR and media agencies exposed to defamation and IP claims.
  • Fund managers and financial advisers whose MAS licence carries a cover condition.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Fee income for the last three years, split by service line
  • Largest five contracts by value and their liability clauses
  • Current policy schedule with retroactive date and limit
  • Claims and notified circumstances for the last five years
  • Standard terms of engagement and professional qualifications
  • Sub-consultant use and whether their work is reviewed

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Profession
Structural engineers and fund managers sit in higher rating bands than HR consultants because claims are larger and more frequent.
Fee income
Insurers rate on annual fees, so a new large contract moves the premium at renewal.
Limit and deductible
The limit demanded by the client contract and the excess the firm accepts set most of the premium.
Retroactive date
Full retroactive cover back to incorporation costs more than cover from inception, because the insurer takes on past work.
Contract terms
Unlimited liability, liquidated damages and fitness-for-purpose clauses in client contracts are underwritten and can be excluded.
Claims history
Claims or notified circumstances in the last five years raise the rate or the deductible.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Retroactive date lost on switching insurer
A firm moves to a cheaper insurer and the new policy starts cover from inception, so claims on earlier work fall into a gap. Require the new policy to carry the old retroactive date before cancelling, and get it printed on the schedule.
Circumstance not notified in time
A client complains in writing, the firm waits to see if it goes away, and the claim arrives after renewal under a new insurer who excludes it as a known circumstance. Notify every written complaint or likely claim to the insurer in the policy year it surfaces.
Policy lapses when the firm closes
The practice winds up, the policy is cancelled, and a claim arrives two years later with no insurer to answer. Buy run-off cover for the period claims can still be brought; for financial loss the Limitation Act allows six years from the cause of action, or three years from discovery, up to a 15-year longstop.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What does claims-made mean?

The policy responds to claims first made against the firm and reported to the insurer during the policy period, not to when the work was done. Work done in 2021 and challenged in 2026 is claimed on the 2026 policy, provided the retroactive date reaches back to 2021. If the policy has lapsed when the claim arrives, there is no cover. Continuous renewal and prompt notification keep the cover intact.

What is a retroactive date?

The earliest date of work the policy will cover. Claims arising from work done before it are excluded. A first-time buyer usually gets a retroactive date equal to inception; a firm with continuous cover keeps its original date at each renewal. When switching insurers, the new policy should carry the same date. The broker checks the schedule for this before the old policy is cancelled.

Which professions must hold it in Singapore?

Advocates and solicitors are covered by the Law Society's compulsory professional indemnity scheme. Retail licensed fund management companies must hold minimum professional indemnity insurance set by MAS, starting at S$2 million for firms managing under S$100 million. Other professions are driven by contract or by a professional body's membership rules rather than statute, so the client's clause is the practical minimum.

How much cover do I need?

Start from the highest limit any current contract demands, then test it against the largest project and the loss a client could claim on it. A design error on a large build can cost many times the fee. Check whether defence costs sit inside or outside the limit; costs inside the limit reduce what is left for the award. Aggregate limits should allow for more than one claim in a year.

Does it cover claims from overseas clients?

Only if the territory and jurisdiction clauses allow it. Many Singapore policies cover work done worldwide but exclude claims brought in USA or Canada courts, or price them separately. A firm with clients in those markets should declare them and ask for the exclusion to be lifted. Contracts governed by foreign law should be listed on the proposal form.

What happens when I retire or close the firm?

Claims can still be made after the firm stops trading. Under section 24A of the Limitation Act, an action for negligence causing financial loss can be brought up to six years from when the cause of action accrued, or three years from when the claimant first had the knowledge needed to sue, with an overriding limit of 15 years under section 24B. Run-off cover keeps a claims-made policy answering through that period.

Sources: MAS: Guidelines on Licensing and Conduct of Business for FMCs, Appendix 3 PII, Limitation Act 1959, sections 24A and 24B, Law Society of Singapore PI scheme (administered by Marsh)
