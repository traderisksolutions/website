The cover

Commercial property insurance in Singapore

Commercial property insurance pays to repair or rebuild a company's buildings, fit-out, plant, stock and contents after accidental physical damage. In Singapore it is written as a fire policy with extra perils, or as an industrial all risks (IAR) policy that covers any accidental cause the wording does not exclude. The purchase is usually forced by a lease that makes the tenant insure its fit-out, a bank that requires cover over a mortgaged unit, or a customer contract that asks for proof of insurance.

It is not machinery breakdown, business interruption or liability cover: an IAR wording excludes a machine's own mechanical or electrical failure and damage done by electrical current. The policy runs for 12 months on an occurrence basis. The buyer declares a sum insured per item at reinstatement (new-for-new) value, accepts a deductible per claim and lists each address. The broker gathers the values, compares insurer wordings and extensions, places the cover and runs the claim with the loss adjuster.

Who buys it

  • Tenants whose lease makes them insure the fit-out, partitions and contents of the unit.
  • Owners of strata industrial or commercial units with a mortgage that requires insurance.
  • Manufacturers and warehouse operators holding plant and stock in Singapore.
  • Retail and F&B chains with several outlets on one schedule.
  • Clinics, laboratories and offices with high-value contents and fit-out.
  • Landlords insuring the shell and common areas of a multi-tenant block.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Address, floor area, construction and year built of each location
  • Reinstatement values split by building, fit-out, plant, stock and contents
  • Occupancy and processes carried out, including any hot work
  • Fire protection in place: sprinklers, alarms, hose reels, extinguishers
  • Three-year claims record with amounts paid
  • Lease or mortgage clauses that set insurance requirements

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Sum insured
Premium is a rate applied to the declared reinstatement value, so the total value of buildings, plant, fit-out and stock sets the base.
Construction and occupancy
A concrete office rates lower than a timber-lined workshop; paint spraying, welding, plastics and cold storage raise the rate.
Fire protection
Sprinklers, hose reels, alarms and a current Fire Safety Certificate reduce the rate; missing fire certification can block cover.
Perils and extensions
Adding flood, landslip and subsidence, theft, plate glass and capital additions widens cover and raises premium.
Deductible
A higher deductible per claim lowers premium; insurers set separate, higher deductibles for flood and water damage.
Claims history
Three years of paid claims and any uninsured losses are declared and rated.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Underinsurance and average
A sum insured below reinstatement value triggers the average clause: the insured bears a proportion of every claim, not only large ones. Revalue each item every year and ask for the 85% average relief clause where the insurer offers it.
Fit-out insured by nobody
A tenant assumes the landlord's building policy covers the partitions, ceilings and cabling it paid for; the landlord's policy covers the shell. Insure fit-out as a separate item at its reinstatement cost.
Breakdown claimed on the wrong policy
A compressor burns out and the property claim is declined because own mechanical or electrical breakdown is excluded. Buy machinery breakdown cover for plant the business cannot run without.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What is the difference between a fire policy and industrial all risks?

A fire policy pays only for the perils it names: fire, lightning, explosion and any extra perils added, such as storm, flood or impact. An industrial all risks policy pays for any accidental physical damage unless the wording excludes it, so burst pipes, accidental breakage and unexplained damage are inside cover. IAR wordings still exclude wear and tear, gradual deterioration, own mechanical breakdown and property in transit.

How is the sum insured set?

On a reinstatement basis: the cost to rebuild or replace with new property of the same kind, including professional fees and debris removal where they are listed as separate items. Market value and book value are the wrong figures. Singapore IAR wordings carry an average clause, so a declared value below the true reinstatement cost reduces every claim in proportion.

Does commercial property insurance cover flood in Singapore?

Flood is a named extra peril, not automatic. Fire policies add it by endorsement; IAR wordings include it unless excluded, often with a separate higher deductible. Landslip and subsidence are usually a further endorsement. Basement units and ground-floor shophouses should confirm the flood deductible in writing before binding.

Does the policy cover stock?

Yes, as a separate item valued at cost price, not selling price. Stock that moves through the year can be insured on a declaration basis: a provisional premium on part of the sum insured, adjusted at expiry against monthly declarations. Stock in transit and stock at a third-party warehouse need their own item or a marine policy.

What does the insurer need after a loss?

Notice as soon as possible, the damaged property kept for inspection, and a written claim with repair quotations or invoices. For larger losses the insurer appoints a loss adjuster who checks the cause, the value at risk and the sum insured. Repairs should not start before the adjuster has seen the damage, except emergency work to stop further loss.

Do I need SCDF approval before renovating an insured unit?

Works that alter fire protection systems, escape routes or compartment walls need SCDF plan approval under the Fire Safety Act 1993, submitted by a Qualified Person. Minor works on the Regulation 4 schedule can be lodged as minor addition or alteration works at S$90 per submission. Tell the insurer before works start: property undergoing construction is excluded under IAR wordings, and the contractor needs its own contractors all risks policy.
