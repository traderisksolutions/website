The cover

Inland transit insurance in Singapore

Inland transit insurance covers goods while they travel by road or rail within Singapore and, by extension, across the Causeway into West Malaysia. It is bought as an annual policy by distributors, wholesalers, e-commerce fulfilment firms, construction suppliers and manufacturers delivering on their own lorries or through a contracted carrier. The trigger is usually a loss the motor policy refused: commercial vehicle insurance excludes goods carried, and a haulier's trading conditions cap its liability at a few dollars per kilogram.

It is not motor insurance, and it does not cover the vehicle, the driver or liability to other road users. The policy is written on all-risks cargo conditions adapted for land transit, with a limit per vehicle or consignment, a deductible per claim and a rate on annual carried value declared at year end. The broker sets the per-vehicle limit to the highest load, adds overnight and depot storage, confirms the territory covers Malaysia, and settles the conditions that apply to theft from an unattended vehicle.

Who buys it

  • Distributors delivering FMCG, electronics or pharmaceuticals on their own fleet.
  • Last-mile and e-commerce fulfilment operators with high-value parcels per van.
  • Construction and M&E suppliers moving materials to sites across Singapore.
  • Manufacturers trucking finished goods to port or to Johor plants.
  • Businesses using contracted hauliers whose liability is capped by trading conditions.
  • Exhibition and event firms moving equipment between venues.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Description of goods and the highest value on any one vehicle
  • Annual value of goods moved by road
  • Number of vehicles, LTA class and whether owned or hired
  • Carrier contracts and their liability terms
  • Routes, overnight stops and depot storage practices
  • Security measures and transit loss history

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Maximum value per vehicle
The highest load on one lorry sets the limit and most of the rate; splitting high-value loads lowers both.
Goods carried
Electronics, tobacco, liquor and pharmaceuticals rate higher for theft; bulk building materials rate lower.
Annual carried value
Premium is a rate on the value moved in a year, declared and adjusted at expiry.
Own fleet or third-party carrier
Own-fleet cover with vetted drivers rates lower than cover for goods in a subcontracted haulier's hands.
Territory
Island-wide Singapore is the base; extension into West Malaysia adds rate and tighter theft conditions.
Security and loss record
GPS tracking, locked bodies, two-driver rules and past theft claims move the deductible and the rate.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Expecting the motor policy to pay
Commercial vehicle policies exclude goods carried, so a load destroyed in a crash is a cargo loss. Buy inland transit cover and set the limit to the full load, not the average.
Underinsuring the peak load
A S$80,000 limit on a lorry that sometimes carries S$200,000 leaves the difference uninsured. Review the highest consignment each quarter and raise the limit before the season peaks.
Leaving goods overnight outside cover
Theft from an unattended vehicle is commonly excluded unless the vehicle is locked and in a secured yard. Write the overnight procedure into the policy and tell the insurer where vehicles park.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Does my commercial vehicle insurance cover the goods on the lorry?

No. Singapore commercial vehicle policies cover the vehicle and third-party liability; MSIG's commercial vehicle product, for example, lists goods in transit as excluded. The goods need an inland transit or marine cargo policy. If a contracted carrier is used, its liability is limited by its trading conditions, so the owner of the goods still needs its own cover.

How much is a Singapore haulier or forwarder liable for?

Only what its trading conditions say. The Singapore Aircargo Agents Association standard terms, for example, cap liability at the lower of the goods' value, S$5 per gross kilogram or the agent's charges, with an absolute cap of S$100,000 per claim. Road hauliers use similar clauses. A pallet of electronics worth S$50,000 weighing 200 kg would recover S$1,000 under a S$5 per kilogram cap.

Is cover extended into Malaysia?

Yes, by agreement. Insurers extend inland transit cover to West Malaysia for goods trucked across the Causeway or the Second Link, usually with a higher deductible for theft and a condition on overnight parking. Journeys beyond West Malaysia, or into Thailand, need specific agreement and may be written on marine cargo terms instead.

What does an all-risks inland transit policy exclude?

The standard exclusions follow the Institute Cargo Clauses: wilful misconduct, ordinary wear and tear, loss from insufficient packing by the insured, inherent vice, delay, and nuclear risks. Policies add conditions for unattended vehicles, overnight storage and goods on open-top vehicles. War is excluded; strikes, riots and civil commotion can be added back by endorsement.

Are goods covered while stored overnight at a depot?

Only if the policy says so. Transit cover ends when goods are unloaded into a place of storage, so a depot or cross-dock needs a declared storage extension with its own limit and a maximum number of days. Declare every depot and the value held there, and tell the insurer when a new one opens.

What vehicle rules affect the fleet carrying the goods?

LTA classes goods vehicles by maximum laden weight: light up to 3,500 kg, heavy from 3,501 to 16,000 kg and very heavy above 16,000 kg, with a 20-year lifespan. Goods vehicles above 12,000 kg must have speed limiters set to 60 km/h, and the rule is extending to lorries of 3,501 to 12,000 kg between January 2026 and July 2027. Non-compliance is a Traffic Police offence.

Sources: MSIG Singapore commercial vehicle (goods in transit excluded), SAAA standard terms, liability limits, LTA OneMotoring: goods vehicle classes and lifespan, Singapore Police Force: speed limiter regime
