The cover

Commercial fleet and motor insurance in Singapore

Commercial vehicle insurance covers vans, lorries, pick-ups, prime movers and company cars used for business, and the liability the business carries for how they are driven. Singapore law makes third-party cover compulsory under the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act: a vehicle cannot be registered with LTA or used on the road without it. Logistics firms, contractors, distributors and service companies move to a fleet policy once they run several vehicles, because one policy, one renewal date and fleet rating replace a stack of single-vehicle covers.

It is not cover for the goods carried, which need inland transit insurance, nor for vehicles used for hire and reward such as ride-hailing, which insurers exclude. Cover is bought as comprehensive, third-party fire and theft, or third-party only, with unlimited third-party bodily injury, a property damage limit, and excesses that rise for young or inexperienced drivers. The broker rates the fleet on its claims record, sets driver and excess terms that fit the roster, and handles claims and recoveries.

Who buys it

  • Logistics and last-mile operators running 10 or more vans and lorries.
  • Construction and M&E contractors with lorries, tippers and crane trucks above 3,500 kg.
  • Distributors and FMCG firms with refrigerated vans and delivery trucks.
  • Companies with pooled cars and vans driven by any employee.
  • Courier firms with motorcycle fleets that a personal policy will not cover.
  • Leasing and rental operators needing cover on vehicles they own, lease or rent out.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Vehicle list with registration, make, laden weight, year and value
  • Claims experience from current insurers, usually three years
  • Driver list with ages and licence dates, or any-driver confirmation
  • Use of each vehicle: goods carried, routes, Malaysia trips
  • Existing policy schedules, NCD entitlements and excess levels
  • Telematics, dashcams or driver training in place

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Fleet size and claims record
Fleets of 10 or more vehicles are rated on their own loss history; MSIG, for example, offers a 20% discount at that size, and a claim-free year earns a refund with some insurers.
Vehicle class and weight
Prime movers and lorries above 16,000 kg carry higher third-party exposure and rate above light goods vehicles under 3,500 kg.
Driver profile
Insurers add an excess for drivers under a stated age or with less than two years on a licence; a named-driver fleet can price lower than any-driver cover.
Cover level
Comprehensive costs most; third-party only is available for older vehicles, with MSIG writing it beyond 25 years of age subject to underwriting.
Vehicle age and value
Own-damage premium follows market value including COE; a vehicle near its 20-year statutory lifespan is usually insured third-party only.
Territory and use
Regular trips into West Malaysia, carriage of hazardous goods or hire-and-reward use change the rate or make the vehicle unplaceable.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Using a personal policy for business use
A private car or motorcycle policy does not cover commercial use, and the General Insurance Association warns the insurer can repudiate the claim and cancel the policy. Insure every vehicle used for work on a commercial policy, including staff motorcycles used for deliveries.
Ignoring the young-driver excess
Policies add a large excess when the driver is under the stated age or has held a licence under two years; Etiqa's is S$4,000 for drivers under 27 or over 75. Match the roster to the policy and negotiate the excess before a new hire drives.
Assuming property damage is unlimited
Only third-party bodily injury is unlimited; property damage is capped, at S$500,000 on several Singapore commercial vehicle products. A heavy vehicle hitting a bus stop, gantry or shop front can exceed that, so raise the limit for lorries and prime movers.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What is the minimum insurance a commercial vehicle needs in Singapore?

Third-party cover for death or bodily injury, required under the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act before LTA registers the vehicle or it is used on the road. Third-party only policies meet the minimum; they do not pay for damage to the insured vehicle. Most fleets buy comprehensive on newer vehicles and third-party only on vehicles near the end of their 20-year lifespan.

What is the difference between comprehensive, TPFT and third-party only?

All three cover unlimited third-party bodily injury and third-party property damage up to the policy limit. Third-party only stops there. Third-party fire and theft adds loss of the insured vehicle by fire or theft. Comprehensive adds accidental damage to the vehicle, windscreen, towing and usually flood. Excesses and young-driver loadings apply only to the own-damage sections.

How does fleet rating work?

From about 10 vehicles the insurer prices the fleet on its own claims record rather than the standard tariff, and the policy covers vehicles added and removed during the year on declaration. MSIG offers a 20% discount for fleets of 10 or more, and some insurers refund part of the premium after a claim-free year. No-claim discount on individual vehicles is replaced by the fleet experience rating.

Are my vehicles covered in Malaysia?

Commercial vehicle policies in Singapore cover use in West Malaysia, and several extend to Thailand within 80 km of the Malaysian border. Conditions differ: Etiqa applies an extra S$2,000 excess if the vehicle is stolen outside Singapore. Check the policy schedule before sending lorries across the Causeway, and confirm the goods in transit policy extends the same way.

Which LTA rules change the premium or the terms?

LTA classes goods vehicles by maximum laden weight: light up to 3,500 kg, heavy 3,501 to 16,000 kg, very heavy above 16,000 kg. Goods vehicles have a 20-year statutory lifespan. Vehicles above 12,000 kg need speed limiters set at 60 km/h, extending to lorries of 3,501 to 12,000 kg between January 2026 and July 2027, and vehicles up to 3,500 kg need a speed warning device. Insurers rate by class and ask about compliance.

What excess will the business pay on a claim?

A standard own-damage excess per claim, a windscreen excess of S$100 on most products, and an additional excess for young or inexperienced drivers: S$4,000 at Etiqa for drivers under 27, over 75 or licensed under two years; Singlife applies it to drivers aged 24 and below or licensed under two years. Third-party claims carry no excess. MSIG writes its fleet cover with no additional driver excess.
