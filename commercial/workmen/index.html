The cover

Work injury compensation (WICA) in Singapore

Work injury compensation insurance pays the compensation an employer owes under the Work Injury Compensation Act (WICA) when an employee is injured or falls ill because of work. It is a no-fault scheme: the employee does not have to prove negligence. Every employer in Singapore with a manual worker, or a non-manual worker earning S$2,600 a month or less, must hold a policy. Main contractors, tenderers and landlords ask for the certificate before work starts, and MOM checks it when an accident is reported.

It is not medical insurance for illness unrelated to work and it is not personal accident cover. The policy is annual, bought on an estimated wage roll and headcount, and must be issued by an MOM-designated insurer on compulsory terms. Payouts follow the WICA scale, so the cover does not have a sum insured; the variable is the common law limit, the sum the insurer pays if an employee sues outside WICA instead. The broker fixes the occupation classes, declares the wages, adds non-compulsory staff where it pays to, and runs the claim from the accident report to the payment.

Who buys it

  • Any employer with a manual worker, from construction crews to kitchen staff and warehouse hands
  • Employers of non-manual staff earning S$2,600 a month or less, including part-timers
  • Main contractors and subcontractors who must show a current policy before site entry
  • Employers of Work Permit and S Pass holders doing manual work, local and foreign alike
  • Platform operators, who must insure platform workers under WICA from 1 January 2025
  • Offices that choose to cover every employee so the WICA claim and the common law suit sit with one insurer

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Employee list by occupation, with manual and non-manual split
  • Annual wage roll for each class, excluding overtime and bonuses
  • Accident and claims history for the past three years
  • Main activities, sites and any work at height, hot work or offshore
  • Current policy schedule and expiry date, if renewing
  • Work Permit and S Pass headcount with nationalities

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Occupation class
Manual trades such as construction, marine and logistics are rated several times higher than office staff.
Declared wage roll
Premium is a rate on the annual wages declared; a low declaration is adjusted at year end or at claim.
Claims history
Three years of accident reports and MOM notices decide whether the insurer loads, excludes or declines.
Common law limit
Raising the limit from S$10 million to S$15 million or more adds premium; the WICA layer itself is fixed by law.
Headcount and turnover
Large or fast-changing crews pay for the flexibility of an unnamed policy adjusted on declaration.
Safety record
A bizSAFE level, a WSH officer and a documented site programme support a lower rate at renewal.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Insuring only the compulsory group
An uninsured employee who earns above S$2,600 can still claim under WICA, and the employer pays the award from its own funds. Cover the whole payroll; the premium for office staff is small against a death award of up to S$269,000.
Under-declaring the wage roll
A declared wage roll below the true figure leaves the insurer entitled to adjust or to scale the payout. Declare actual wages at inception and update when headcount changes, not only at renewal.
Missing the 10-day report
A non-fatal accident with medical leave or light duty must be reported to MOM within 10 days of first notice; a fatal one within 10 days of the accident. Set the report as the site manager's task on the day of the accident, and copy the broker.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

Who must I insure under WICA?

All employees doing manual work, whatever their salary, and all non-manual employees earning S$2,600 a month or less. The salary test uses basic pay and excludes overtime, bonus, annual wage supplement, productivity incentives and allowances. Local and foreign employees count equally. Other employees may be insured at your choice, but a valid WICA claim from them must still be paid.

What are the compensation limits in 2026?

For accidents from 1 November 2025: medical expenses up to S$53,000 or one year from the accident, whichever comes first; permanent incapacity between S$116,000 and S$346,000 scaled by the percentage of incapacity; death between S$91,000 and S$269,000. Medical leave wages are full average monthly earnings for 14 outpatient days or 60 hospitalisation days, then two-thirds up to one year.

What happens if I have no policy?

Failing to insure is an offence with a fine of up to S$10,000, jail of up to 12 months, or both. The compensation itself is still owed. MOM assesses the claim and serves a notice of assessment on the employer, who pays from company funds.

Does the policy cover a lawsuit by the employee?

WICA compensation and a common law claim are alternatives; an employee may sue for negligence instead of claiming under WICA. Policies in Singapore add a common law section for this, with limits in insurer product sheets of S$10 million to S$15 million. Check the limit matches the contract or tender requirement before you sign.

Is an accident overseas or at home covered?

Yes, where it arises out of and in the course of work. MOM lists accidents on an overseas assignment and accidents during agreed work-from-home or flexible arrangements as eligible. Travel to and from work on normal commuting is not, unless it is company transport. The employer still reports within 10 days.

How fast must I pay after an accident?

Medical leave wages are due by the next payday after you receive the original medical certificates, even while the insurer is still processing. Medical bills are paid direct to the hospital, or reimbursed to the employee within 14 days of receiving the original bill. The insurer refunds you. The employee can claim up to one year after the accident.
