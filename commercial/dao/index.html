The cover

Directors and officers insurance in Singapore

Directors and officers insurance pays the defence costs, investigation costs and damages that directors, officers and senior managers face personally when they are sued or investigated over decisions made in running the company. Singapore buyers include private companies with outside investors, subsidiaries of foreign groups, SGX-listed companies and firms licensed by MAS or MOH. The purchase is usually triggered by a funding round, a board appointment, a listing or a regulator's enquiry. Investors and independent directors often make it a condition of joining.

It is not cover for the company's trade debts, nor for fraud once proven. Under section 172 of the Companies Act, any provision exempting or indemnifying an officer against liability for negligence, default, breach of duty or breach of trust to the company is void; section 172A lets the company buy insurance instead. The policy is claims-made with one annual aggregate limit shared across Side A (the individual), Side B (company reimbursement) and Side C (the entity, usually for securities claims). The broker sets the limit, the retentions and the extensions.

Who buys it

  • Companies raising venture or private equity funding where investors require cover before closing.
  • Boards appointing independent or non-executive directors who ask for protection.
  • Singapore subsidiaries of foreign groups whose local directors are not on the parent's policy.
  • Fintech, payments and healthcare firms licensed by MAS or MOH and open to regulatory investigation.
  • Employers exposed to wrongful dismissal, discrimination and harassment claims.
  • Companies near insolvency where a liquidator may pursue former directors.

What the insurer needs to quote

  • Latest audited accounts and current management accounts
  • Shareholder list and any venture or private equity terms
  • Board and senior management list with appointment dates
  • Any litigation, regulatory enquiry or shareholder dispute, open or threatened
  • Group structure including overseas subsidiaries
  • Employee headcount and any dismissal or harassment complaints

Price and terms

What moves the premium

Balance sheet
Insurers read net assets, cash runway and debt; a weak balance sheet raises the chance of insolvency claims and the premium.
Listing and investors
An SGX listing or a US-listed parent adds securities-claim exposure; private companies with no outside shareholders pay less.
Sector and regulator
MAS-licensed, MOH-licensed and digital asset businesses carry investigation exposure that is rated higher.
Limit structure
The limit chosen, and whether Side A is ring-fenced or shared with the entity, sets most of the premium.
Open matters
Current litigation, regulatory notices or shareholder disputes are excluded from the new policy and priced into the rest.
US exposure
Employees, subsidiaries or listings in the USA bring US litigation costs, which are priced separately or excluded.

At renewal

Three mistakes we see most often

Side A shared away
The company reimbursement and entity sections exhaust the limit on a securities or employment claim, leaving the directors with nothing when the company cannot indemnify them. Buy a dedicated Side A excess limit or a reinstatement clause so the individuals keep a limit of their own.
Insolvency arrives after the policy lapses
The company stops paying premiums as cash runs out, and the liquidator sues former directors months later with no policy in force. Keep the policy running and buy a run-off period before winding up; the insolvent company is the one most likely to produce a claim.
Known circumstances not declared
A regulator's letter or a shareholder's threat is left off the proposal form, the policy is placed, and the claim is declined for non-disclosure. Declare every open matter in writing; the insurer excludes it, and the rest of the cover stands.

Questions

Questions buyers ask

What do Side A, Side B and Side C mean?

Side A pays the director or officer directly when the company cannot or will not indemnify them, for example in insolvency or where the Companies Act forbids the indemnity. Side B reimburses the company when it has lawfully indemnified a director. Side C covers the company itself, usually only for securities claims. All three share one aggregate limit unless a separate Side A limit is bought.

Can the company not just indemnify its directors?

Only in part. Section 172 of the Companies Act makes void any provision that exempts or indemnifies an officer against liability for negligence, default, breach of duty or breach of trust in relation to the company. Section 172A allows the company to buy and maintain insurance against that liability, and section 172B permits a narrower third-party indemnity. The policy fills the gap the Act leaves.

Does it cover regulatory investigations by MAS or ACRA?

Most policies pay legal representation costs when a director is required to attend, or is named in, a formal investigation by a regulator, and some extend to internal inquiries that precede a self-report. Civil fines and penalties may be covered where the law allows; criminal fines are not. Read the investigation extension and its sub-limit before placement.

Are former directors covered?

Yes, where the policy defines insured persons to include past, present and future directors and officers, which most do. The claim must be made while a policy is in force, so a director who has left still depends on the company renewing. A departing director can ask for a run-off period or a contractual promise to maintain cover for a set number of years.

Does it cover claims by the company against its own directors?

Usually not. The insured-versus-insured exclusion removes claims brought by the company or on its behalf, with carve-backs for liquidators, whistleblowers and shareholder derivative actions. The liquidator carve-back matters: in 2024 the High Court held a former director of Inter-Pacific Petroleum liable for US$146 million plus interest for breach of duty. Check the carve-back wording.

What does employment practices liability add?

Cover for claims by employees or former employees alleging wrongful dismissal, discrimination, harassment or retaliation, against both the individuals and the company. It is bought as an extension or a separate policy with its own sub-limit and retention. In Singapore, salary and dismissal disputes first go to the Tripartite Alliance for Dispute Management and the Employment Claims Tribunals; larger claims go to court.

Sources: Companies Act 1967, sections 172 to 172B, ACRA: key changes to the Companies Act affecting directors and CEOs
